What could go wrong?

Pick a bad night. See which policy shows up.

The same few things go wrong in restaurants again and again. Here's which coverage usually responds and where the gaps are.

A guest slips on a wet floor

Someone goes down by the bus station on a Friday night, hurts a wrist and later sues.

Usually respondsGeneral liability

Watch outIf it's your server who slips, that's a workers' comp claim, not general liability.9

A grease fire shuts the kitchen

The hood catches, the line is ruined, and you're closed for weeks while it's repaired.

Usually respondsProperty + business income

Watch outLost income is its own coverage, with its own limit and waiting period. Know those numbers before you need them.

A bartender over-serves a guest

An obviously drunk guest gets another round, drives off and hurts someone.

Usually respondsLiquor liability

Watch outGeneral liability usually doesn't cover alcohol-related claims.2 In California, serving an obviously intoxicated person is a misdemeanor. Sellers are generally shielded from civil suits over what an intoxicated adult does,7 but not when the guest is an obviously intoxicated minor.8 And being shielded doesn't stop someone from suing, so you still need a defense.

The walk-in dies overnight

The power goes out or the compressor quits, and a week of product is warm by morning.

Usually respondsSpoilage / equipment breakdown

Watch outThis is usually an add-on (endorsement) to your property policy or BOP, not automatic.2

A line cook gets burned

A fryer accident means a hospital visit and weeks off the line.

Usually respondsWorkers' comp

Watch outIn California this is required once you have one employee, part-time included.4 Going without can bring a stop order, a $1,500 penalty per uninsured employee,6 and a misdemeanor charge.5

A guest says your food made them sick

A customer gets sick after dinner and blames the kitchen.

Usually respondsGeneral liability (products)

Watch outProduct liability is typically part of general liability. Spoiled inventory is a separate property coverage.2

A delivery run ends in a crash

Your driver rear-ends someone on the way to a catering drop.

Usually respondsCommercial auto or hired & non-owned auto

Watch outCommercial auto is for vehicles the business owns. If staff drive their own cars for work, ask about hired and non-owned auto.2

A former employee sues over a firing

A server you let go claims it was wrongful termination.

Usually respondsEmployment practices liability (EPLI)

Watch outEPLI pays legal costs when an employee alleges wrongful termination or a similar violation.2 General liability isn't built for this.

Insurance words, translated

The fine print, in plain English.

Per occurrence / aggregate

The most a policy pays for one claim, and the most it pays in total for the policy period. Often written as $1M / $2M.1

Deductible

The part of a covered loss you pay before the insurance pays.

BOP (business owner's policy)

A package that bundles general liability and property, often with business income.2

Endorsement

An add-on that changes a policy, like adding spoilage coverage.

Certificate of insurance (COI)

A one-page proof of coverage that landlords, event venues and vendors ask for.

Additional insured

Someone else, often your landlord, added to your policy for claims tied to your operations.

Dram shop

Laws about a seller's liability for serving alcohol. California's are in Business & Professions Code §25602 and §25602.1.7

Questions owners ask me

Restaurant insurance questions, short answers.

Do you work with new restaurants and ownership changes?

Yes. I work with owners opening a new restaurant and owners taking over an existing one, including programs built for new restaurants.

Which cities do you serve?

What coverage do restaurants and bars need?

Most restaurants, bars, breweries and cafés look at general liability, liquor liability if they serve alcohol, property, and workers' comp for their staff. I'll help you figure out what fits your place.

Is workers' comp required for a restaurant in California?

Yes, if you have even one employee, part-time included.4 California law requires every employer to carry workers' compensation, and going without it can bring stop orders, penalties and criminal charges.5,6

Does general liability cover alcohol-related claims?

Usually not. General liability usually doesn't cover alcohol-related incidents, which is why restaurants, bars and breweries that serve beer, wine or spirits look at liquor liability.2

Can a California restaurant or bar be sued over a guest’s drinking?

Yes, in some cases. California’s statute makes a licensee liable for serving an obviously intoxicated minor,8 and the state ABC notes that negligent or reckless service can still lead to a lawsuit under common law, whatever the guest’s age.10 That’s the kind of claim liquor liability is built for.
