The Donut Man, Glendora: a walk-up window on Route 66 that never closes, and the strawberry doughnut that made it famous

Jim and Miyoko Nakano started frying here in 1972. Fifty-four years later the window is still open all night, and people still drive across the county for what comes out of it.

The way the Los Angeles Times told it in 2011, Elvis Presley was “a jelly man,” and he sent his karate instructor to Glendora to pick up raspberry-filled doughnuts. Roy Rogers ordered a buttermilk bar before heading out on hunting trips. The paper filed those stories under lore, and the shop they belong to is not much to look at: a takeout window and a few benches on a stretch of old Route 66. 1

People still line up there at 10 p.m. 2 The reason is a doughnut that needs a fork.

Why you should go

Order the fruit that’s in season. The famous one is the fresh strawberry doughnut: a big glazed shell split like a clamshell and stuffed with whole berries in a house-made glaze. The Times called it “a clamshell of fried dough stuffed with a gooey mess of strawberries,” and the standard advice is to eat it with a fork. 12 NBC Los Angeles listed it at $6.50 each when the 2025 season opened. 3 The berries usually come back in January or February and run for months, typically wrapping up in the fall. Fresh peach takes a turn in midsummer. 32

Going this month? Strawberry season has typically ended by November, but NBC Los Angeles calls the pumpkin doughnut a fall favorite at the shop. 4 Add a tiger tail, the foot-long glazed twist with a ribbon of chocolate, and a Bavarian cream or maple bar if you want what the Times said draws raves. 1 The case changes with the fruit, so check @donutmanca before you drive, since the shop announces each new season on social media. 3

When to go. Any time. The Glendora shop is open 24 hours a day, seven days a week, closing only for some major holidays. 52 A 2026 guide says weekend mornings in strawberry season bring the longest waits, with reports of half-hour lines, and that late nights are the local workaround because the line moves. 2

What to expect. You order at a walk-up window. There’s no indoor seating, just a few outdoor benches and a small lot. 62 That same guide says to bring cash and patience. 2

What’s different. The shop says it is home of the original fresh strawberry and peach stuffed doughnut, and it has never franchised. In 54 years it added exactly one more counter, at Grand Central Market in downtown L.A., in 2020. 57 Nakano told the Times the base of his doughnuts depends on potato flour, wet yeast and clean shortening, and that he breaks open doughnuts to check them: “I’m very hardheaded; I won’t scrimp.” 1

The backstory: “I like hot doughnuts”

Jim Nakano was born in Boyle Heights in 1940. When he was 2, his family was sent to the incarceration camp at Poston, Arizona. After the war they moved through San Pedro and East Los Angeles. He studied marketing at Cal State L.A., served in the Navy, and became a department manager at J.C. Penney. 18

He met Miyoko on a train in Europe, and they married eight months later. In the early 1970s he doubted a store manager’s pay would ever buy a house, so they talked about going into business. As he told Cal State L.A.’s magazine, “She said, ‘I like hot doughnuts.’” 19 He studied bakeries and French pastry, then bought a Foster’s Donuts franchise in Glendora in May 1972. He worked the fryer and she worked the window. 951

The strawberry doughnut arrived in 1974. Glendora was surrounded by strawberry farms then, and the shop’s own history says a local farmer mentioned he had plenty of berries and told Jim to find a use for them. Nakano told NBC News a grower friend said, “Jim, you gotta start a strawberry donut.” 589 Later accounts describe a produce vendor with a bumper crop instead. 2 According to the Times, a friend who owned a Marie Callender’s helped perfect the glaze. Peach came next, then pumpkin. 1

The Times and Cal State L.A. date the shop’s independence to 1985. The shop’s own history says the mid-’80s. Either way, the name came from a little girl who spotted Jim at a restaurant: “It’s the doughnut man!” 195 A 1999 PBS segment brought fans from outside the valley. Later came the Today show and Food Network’s The Best Thing I Ever Ate. 15 In 2025 the shop made The64’s bracket for best doughnut in America. 10

His philosophy hasn’t needed updating:

“I’ll kick up the price, but I won’t lower my quality. Mainly because I eat the doughnuts too.” (Jim Nakano, to the Los Angeles Times)

If you go

The Donut Man is at 915 E. Route 66 in Glendora, just off the 210. Pick your hour: a pumpkin doughnut on a November evening, a tiger tail after a late shift, or strawberry season in the new year with a fork in the glovebox. The window will be open.
