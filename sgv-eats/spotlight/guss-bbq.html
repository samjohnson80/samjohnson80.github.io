Gus’s BBQ, South Pasadena: 80 years on Route 66, and the baby back ribs that outlived a six-month prediction

Three relatives drove west from Cleveland and opened Gus’s on Fair Oaks Avenue in 1946. In 2008 the new owners bet it on barbecue. Go for the Memphis ribs and the skillet cornbread.

On the second day after Gus’s reopened in 2008, a customer grabbed the new owner by the shirt. As John Bicos told Eater LA, the man said he’d be “out of business in six months.” 1

That was 18 years ago. Gus’s BBQ opened on Fair Oaks Avenue in 1946, which makes 2026 its 80th year, and Eater has called it “perhaps Los Angeles County’s oldest barbecue restaurant.” 1 The South Pasadena restaurant is the original; Gus’s has since added locations in Claremont and Porter Ranch. 4

Why you should go

Order the Memphis baby back ribs. They’re brushed with Gus’s original barbecue sauce: five bones for $25.95, eight for $32.95, or a full rack for $38.95. 5 Back in 2008, Los Angeles Times critic S. Irene Virbila wrote that she knew people who had gone back twice in the same week for them, describing a full slab “at least a foot long, lean and meaty, basted to a dark mahogany.” 2 If you prefer a dry rub, St. Louis spare ribs are the same price. 5

Start with the cornbread. The cast iron cornbread ($8.95) comes with jalapeño jelly and honey butter. 5 Virbila listed it among the best dishes and described it as “coarse-grained as polenta,” arriving in the skillet “under a blanket of fused sugar.” 2 Pulled pork nachos ($16.95) and BBQ sliders ($15.95) are the other shareable starters. 5

Then pick a second meat. The Texas prime beef brisket is dry-rubbed and smoked for up to 14 hours ($27.95 for 7 ounces), and the Carolina-style pulled pork is $22.95. 65 Reviews have not always agreed here. In 2008 the Times found the brisket “beefy but boring” and the pulled pork drier than it should be. 2 That was before the menu was rebuilt around barbecue; today the South Pasadena Chamber of Commerce’s listing says the meats are seasoned overnight and smoked over whole pecan logs, with rubs and sauces made from scratch daily. 3 Diners on OpenTable rate the place 4.8 out of 5 across more than 2,000 reviews. 5

When to go. Gus’s is open every day: 11 a.m. on weekdays, 9 a.m. on weekends, when there’s breakfast until 2 p.m. Happy hour runs Monday through Friday from 3 to 5:30 p.m. 4 It’s a sit-down room with a full bar, and you can book a table on OpenTable. 5

What’s different. It’s an 80-year-old room that doesn’t act like a museum. The Bicos brothers kept the history on the walls and changed the cooking, and Eater’s headline put it this way: L.A.’s oldest barbecue restaurant “isn’t stuck in time.” 12

The backstory: Route 66, a diner, and a sign in a yard

In 1946, Gus and Jack Tripodes and their relative Mike left the family-owned bar in Cleveland and drove west on Route 66, which then ran through South Pasadena. 37 Eater and the Los Angeles Times describe the third partner as a brother-in-law. 12 On Fair Oaks Avenue they bought a little diner called Hamburger Mac’s, named the new place after Gus, the eldest, and hung out what the Times later called “the now familiar red and green sign.” 72

The Tripodes family ran it for six decades. Peter Tripodes started working there in 1955, at 13, and later co-owned Gus’s with his brother-in-law, Dino Lappas. He retired in 2007 after more than 50 years at the restaurant. 8 By then, John Bicos told Eater, Gus’s had “really become a diner essentially.” It still had ribs and barbecue chicken, “but no smoker.” 1

That year brothers Chris and John Bicos bought it. Their family has run the Original Tops in Pasadena since it opened in 1952, and John had grown up eating at Gus’s. 1 The tie was personal, too:

“The best man at my wedding, his great-grandfather was Gus himself.” John Bicos, to Eater LA 1

They closed Gus’s in October 2007, refinished the room, put up framed photos of Gus and company in the old days, and reopened in April 2008 with more barbecue on the menu. 2 The brothers toured the country eating smoked meat and taking notes, and PBS SoCal reports they still travel each year tasting barbecue to bring back. 17 Virbila’s verdict in the Times: “It’s actually better than it was.” 2

One relic made it back. Old photos from around 1952 showed a small neon cocktail sign hanging under the blade sign out front. The Tripodes family didn’t know where it went, but the company that made it remembered the job and still had the sign sitting in its yard. It was beat up, Bicos said, “but it still worked,” so they cleaned it up and hung it in the back room. 1

If you go

Gus’s is at 808 Fair Oaks Avenue, at Mission Street, in South Pasadena. Come at 3 for happy hour or on a weekend morning for breakfast. Order the cornbread for the table and a rack of Memphis baby backs, and look for the old cocktail sign in the back room. The man who gave it six months was off by about 18 years and counting.
