Walter’s, Claremont: an Afghan pilot’s coffee shop, two fires, and Kabuli pilaf back on Yale Avenue

The Ghafarshad family has run Walter’s since 1973. After two years and two months closed, it reopened on Oct. 14, 2026. Go for the Afghan classics and stay for the cakes.

The ad was in the Los Angeles Times: a small coffee shop for sale in Claremont. The man who answered it had flown for the Afghan Air Force and was teaching flying at Brackett Field. In 1973 he and his wife paid $24,000 for it. 1

That was Walter’s. Fifty-three years and two fires later, Nangy and Fahima Ghafarshad still own it, and on Oct. 14, 2026, after more than two years closed, it opened its doors again. 56

Why you should go

Order from the Afghan classics. The menu runs from steak frites to pasta, but the Afghan dishes are where the story is. Start with the Kabuli pilaf ($29): slow-braised lamb over rice pilaf with caramelized carrots and raisins, served with grilled eggplant and tomato, garlic yogurt, and fresh mint. 7 Vegetarian? The badenjan boranie ($25) puts grilled eggplant, tomato, yogurt, and dried mint over the same carrot-and-raisin pilaf. 7

Get starters for the table. The bolawnies ($16) are what the menu calls “our signature take on an Afghan classic”: wontons filled with ground beef, potatoes, and green onions, fried and served with garlic yogurt. Add the Afghan fries ($13), beer-battered potato rounds with the same yogurt sauce. At dinner there’s also aash ($14), angel hair noodles with garbanzo and kidney beans and seasoned beef in a warm yogurt broth with dried mint. 7

Save room for dessert. The Claremont Courier calls Fahima Ghafarshad’s desserts “legendary.” 2 The dessert menu lists a Pear Charlotte (ladyfinger cake with poached pears, chocolate mousse, and pear brandy Bavarian cream), a gluten-free Concord cake of chocolate meringue and mousse, and an apricot tarte on a marzipan crust, each $16. Can’t pick? A small platter of cookies, brownie, and baklava is $21. 7

When to go. Walter’s is closed Monday. It opens at 11 a.m. Tuesday through Friday and at 9 a.m. on weekends, with brunch Saturday and Sunday (eggs Benedict, omelets, Belgian waffles). 67 Happy hour runs Tuesday through Friday from 3 to 6 p.m., with tandoori wings at $11, and the new bar and lounge stays open until 1:30 a.m. Thursday through Sunday. 67

What to expect. A restaurant that is still finding its feet. The family chose to reopen in weekly phases, adding hours, rooms, and menu items as staff are hired and trained, and plans a grand reopening near the end of this year or early next. 56 The menu is a bit smaller than before, prices went up, and the fresh-baked bread at the table now costs extra; it’s $5 for six slices. 57 The front dining room is now on one level, with a new lounge, booths along the windows, and a redone sidewalk patio. 5 You can book a table on the restaurant’s website. 6

The backstory: a coffee shop, a bucket, and two fires

Walter Boldig opened Walter’s Coffee Shop in 1957. It seated 35 and shared a modern cement-block building with Hendricks Pharmacy, designed by local architect Foster Rhodes Jackson. 1 Claremont attorney Herb Hafif, a friend of Boldig’s, was developing the property. 8

Nangy Ghafarshad had come to the United States with Fahima for pilot training and decided not to go back. They arrived with a young daughter, Nangy’s brother, and only a few dollars. 1 When they bought the coffee shop, they “changed a perfectly good menu,” as the Courier put it. In his memoir, Nangy wrote that one customer asked him “if the Afghan burger was made with Afghan hound’s meat?” Within days the old regulars were gone. College students and professors kept it going, and taught the couple what to serve. 1

The Ghafarshads added a patio, then a beer and wine license, then a bungalow next door as a new dining room. 1 Eventually Walter’s took over the whole bungalow court. Nangy dug a wine cellar and bakery beneath the bungalows himself, one bucket at a time, over two years. 2

In 1987, a 2 a.m. call brought them to a restaurant engulfed in flames. The kitchen staff came back and offered to work without pay, and neighbors brought lunch for the crew. Hafif, by then the landlord, helped cover what insurance didn’t, and his wife, Kay, introduced Fahima to Austrian and French pastries. 28 The rebuilt Walter’s grew into a restaurant, bar, lounge, and event space for 500, and for years it was the weekly meeting place of artists Sam Maloof, Harrison McIntosh, Rupert Deese, and James Heuter. 2

The family celebrated 50 years of ownership in late 2023. 1 Then, shortly before 2:33 a.m. on Aug. 22, 2024, fire broke out again, mostly in the roof. No one was hurt. A fire captain estimated repairs at more than $500,000 and pointed to a possible air-conditioning failure, with the cause under investigation. 3 Three months later, Dawoud Ghafarshad, the couple’s son and general manager, put the damage at “easily over $1 million” and said the insurers’ view was a greasy rag near the water heater. 24

The rebuild took more than two years. Dawoud told the Courier that the renovation, code upgrades, lost revenue, and wages “after the insurance had reached its limits” cost far more than planned. 5

“If a steak is undercooked, we can fix that in 10 minutes. But if something needs to be fixed with the physical restaurant itself, it could take a month — or 10 months.” Dawoud Ghafarshad, to the Claremont Courier 5

“We want to create the next era of Walter’s Restaurant — familiar, relevant, and here for the long run,” Dawoud said. 5

If you go

Walter’s is at 310 N. Yale Avenue in Claremont Village. Come any day but Monday, or make it a weekend brunch. Order the Kabuli pilaf, put bolawnies in the middle of the table, and finish with a slice of Pear Charlotte. Then tell them you’re glad they’re back.
