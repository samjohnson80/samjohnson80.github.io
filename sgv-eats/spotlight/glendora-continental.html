Glendora Continental

A sheepherder who hated being outside and a cook trained in a Basque hotel from age 12 opened this Route 66 restaurant in 1980. It still serves rack of lamb, pickled tongue and Picon Punch, with bingo on Tuesdays.

On a Saturday night along Route 66 in Glendora, a cover band plays the Kinks on a small stage, guests in cowboy hats and baseball caps work through lamb shank and prime rib, and several birthdays happen at once. That is how the Los Angeles Times found Glendora Continental in 2025, calling it a nostalgic time capsule of the local Basque community and one of the last Basque-owned restaurants in California.1

If you have never had Basque food, this is the easy way in. If you have, you already know how few places like it are left.

Why you should go

Order the Basque Set Up. It is the restaurant's multicourse dinner: a cup of soup du jour, pickled tongue, ham, bouchées à la reine, a grilled half rack of lamb, potatoes or rice, cheese and baguette, and flan or chocolate mousse. The Times listed it at $46 in August 2025.1 Prices change, so check when you order.

Or order like a regular. The dishes Elisabeth Sabarots brought from the French Basque Country are still on the menu: slow-braised lamb in a Burgundy demi-glace, bouchée à la reine, pickled tongue and escargots à la bourguignonne. Over the years the family added American plates like crab cakes, grilled steaks and salads.1 Co-owner Antoinette Sabarots described the result this way:1

“I would say it’s Basque with a sprinkle of American, or vice versa.”

Get a Picon Punch. The bar pours French and Californian wines, apéritifs from Ricard to Dubonnet, and the traditional Basque drink called Picon Punch.1

Pick your night. The restaurant serves lunch Monday through Thursday from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. and dinner from 3 to 10 p.m.; Friday is dinner only, 3 to 10; Saturday runs lunch 11 to 4 and dinner 4 to 10; Sunday is breakfast and lunch. The bar and lounge are open seven days, 11 a.m. to 2 a.m., with happy hour Monday through Friday from 3 to 7 (half-off appetizers and drink specials).2 Tuesday is bingo at 6:30 p.m. Wednesday is karaoke and Wine Down Wednesday, with $2 glasses and half-off bottles. Friday and Saturday bring live bands from 9 p.m. to 1 a.m., and Sunday has an open jam from 7 to 11 p.m.25 The Times reported it is open until 2 a.m. every day of the year except Christmas, with Halloween and New Year’s Eve parties.1

What to expect. A nearly 7,000-square-foot room with an old-school diner look, Basque photos and a jai alai carving on the walls, and a crowd that knows each other.1 Paul and Jan Collett told the Times they have eaten there nearly every day for 25 years.1

“We’ve got several friends that we meet down there all the time, so it’s just like home.”

The sheepherder and the hotel cook

Jean Sabarots came to California from the French Basque town of Osses in 1955 and worked as a sheepherder, like many Basque immigrants of his generation.1 The restaurant’s own history says he entered through Coalinga, worked on a dairy farm, and spent his free time in Chino, where Basque families gathered.3 He was not cut out for the hills. “We never liked to camp with him because he hated being outside,” his daughter Antoinette told the Times. “I can only imagine he herded sheep as minimally as he could.”1 By 1962 he was bartending at the Can Can Club in Covina.1

On a trip home in 1964 he met Elisabeth Larralde, who had worked at the Hotel Arcé in Saint-Étienne-de-Baïgorry for more than a decade, starting at 12. They married in Chino in 1966, worked at Covina’s Little Inn Lounge and Smorgasbord, eventually owned it, and saved enough to open Glendora Continental. They ran both until the Little Inn closed in 1989.1 Jean ran the bar. Elisabeth hosted and ran the kitchen.1

The restaurant’s site and the Times both put the opening in 1980.13 A 2012 obituary for Jean in the Basque-American paper Euskal Kazeta gives 1985, and notes the many Basque weddings and parties held there over the years.4

What stayed

Elisabeth died in 2005. Their daughter Bernadette moved back to Glendora to work beside her father until he died in 2012.1 She is now general manager and co-owner.1 Lunchtime chef Marcelino Espinoza trained under Elisabeth and has been there since the restaurant opened.1 The three daughters grew up there, washing dishes, whipping up chocolate mousse and folding napkins.1

In 2025 the family put the restaurant up for sale and began considering offers, the Times reported.1 No sale has been reported in the sources cited here, and the restaurant’s site still lists its hours and its weekly bingo, karaoke, band and jam nights.25 Marguerite Jaureguy, Jean’s companion in his last years, told the Times what he used to say:1

“He used to say in Basque, ‘Goatzen aitzina,’ which means ‘Let’s move forward’ — and now Bernie says the same thing.”

If you go

Glendora Continental is at 316 W. Route 66 in Glendora, in the foothills of the San Gabriel Mountains.12 Bring an appetite and order the Set Up, or come for happy hour and a Picon Punch and stay for the band. Get directions on Google Maps.
