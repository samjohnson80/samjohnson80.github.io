Old Sasoon Bakery, Pasadena: a 1948 Aleppo bakery, a James Beard nod, and lahmajune for $2.50

The Geragosian family has baked Armenian and Syrian flatbreads on Allen Avenue since 1986. Come before 3 p.m. for the khachapuri.

Price
Flatbreads from $2.25. Khachapuri $15 on the bakery’s Yelp menu.

In February 2022, the James Beard Foundation named 20 semifinalists for Outstanding Baker in America. One entry read “Joseph, Archalous, and Caroline Geragosian, Old Sasoon Bakery, Pasadena, CA.” 1 Pasadena Now put it plainly: three semifinalists for one of the most prestigious awards in food, working “in a nondescript building on Allen Avenue.” 2

That building is a family bakery where a lahmajune, the thin Armenian flatbread topped with ground beef, tomato, onion, parsley, and garlic, is listed at $2.50. 6 Its story starts in Syria, almost 80 years ago.

Why you should go

Order the khachapuri. The Infatuation, which rates Old Sasoon an 8.0, is blunt about it: “If you order one thing at Old Sasoon, let it be this bread boat.” 4 It’s a Georgian flatbread shaped like a canoe, filled with a blend of white cheeses and an egg. The Infatuation says to tear off a piece of the soft golden crust and swirl it through the salty, eggy center, and that one is big enough for two. 4 It’s listed at $15 on the bakery’s Yelp menu, and you can add soujouk, basturma, ham, or bacon. 68

Then build a spread. Almost everything in the case is under $5. On the Yelp menu, a za’atar flatbread is $2.25, a lahmajune is $2.50, spinach, olive, potato, mushroom, and Swiss chard beorags are $3.75 each, and a soujouk and cheese turnover is $4.50. 6 The Infatuation’s favorite pastry is the soujouk and cheese, which it calls so soft and fluffy “that your fingers leave imprints when you press them.” It also likes the jingalov hatz, a flatbread packed with fresh herbs. 4 Want it as a meal? The wraps are $8 and come rolled up with tomato, green olives, onion, and fresh mint. 6

Pro tip. The khachapuri is the breakfast special, so show up before 3 p.m. 5 Order at the counter and expect to wait about ten minutes while the kitchen warms your bread. 4 Use the time to grab a seat. The Infatuation suggests pairing a bowl of foul, the spicy stewed fava beans, with lahmajune: tear the flatbread and make little wraps with the mint, olives, and tomato on the side. 4 On the way out, grab baklava for later. Back in 2008, FoodGPS noted that Joseph limits the sweetness, so it isn’t drowning in syrup. 9

When to go. Old Sasoon opens at 7 a.m. Monday through Saturday. It closes at 6 p.m. on weekdays and 5 p.m. on Saturday, and it’s closed Sunday. 37 Google’s popular-times data shows Saturday from about 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. and Friday lunch as the busiest stretches. Weekday mornings around 7 and 8 a.m. are calmer. 7

What to expect. Nothing fancy. The Infatuation describes a simple gray dining room, a sunny sidewalk patio, and self-serve coffee, with “no music or TV.” 4 The family added the indoor dining room more recently. 5 Everything is made from scratch, according to Pasadena Now. 2

The backstory: Aleppo to Allen Avenue

Haroutioun Geragosian started young. According to a 2008 FoodGPS profile, he went to work in a bakery in Aleppo, Syria, at 13 to help support his family. 9 His son tells it a little differently. “My dad started in the business when he was nine years old,” Joseph told Pasadena Now in 2022. “His father passed away, so he had to do something.” 2

In 1948 Haroutioun opened his own bakery in Aleppo. He named it for Sasoon, the village in Armenia that his grandparents had left. 93 In the 1980s the family moved to the United States, and in 1986 they opened in Pasadena. 2 The first shop was a third of its current size, per Pasadena Now, and FoodGPS reported it sold just lahmajune and cheese and spinach beorags. 29

Joseph runs the bakery day to day. He works alongside his sister Caroline and their mother Archalous. 9 He started baking at 15, and by 2008 he had grown the lineup to 17 kinds of savory bread. 9 His first job in the family bakery in Syria was making cookies, and his favorite things to make are still the sweets. 2 He also added a sign out front in Arabic. It reads “Abou Yousef,” which means “Joseph’s father.” 39

The name matters to them. Other bakeries around the country are called Sasoon Bakery as part of a chain, Joseph told Pasadena Now. His family’s has always been independent. “That’s why we call it ‘Old Sasoon bakery,’” he said. 2

“At almost 40 years old, Old Sasoon Bakery has made an indelible impact on the Armenian dining scene in Los Angeles.” Eater LA 3

If you go

Old Sasoon Bakery is at 1132 N. Allen Avenue in Pasadena. Go on a weekday morning, before 3 p.m. if you want the khachapuri. Bring someone to split the bread boat with, add a couple of lahmajune, and take a box of baklava home for later.
