Fosselman’s Ice Cream, Alhambra: the family soda fountain whose milkshakes The Guardian ranked among the world’s 50 best things to eat

Three generations, one ice cream recipe from Iowa, and every scoop still made on site on Main Street.

In September 2009, The Guardian ranked the 50 best things to eat in the world. Number nine was a milkshake. The place it named was not in London or Paris. It was on Main Street in Alhambra.5 “Make a beeline for the double-chocolate malt,” the paper told its readers.5

That shop is Fosselman’s Ice Cream, a squat brown-and-tan 1905 building that the LA Times found wedged between a bank and a car dealership.2 The family that runs it started making ice cream in Iowa more than a century ago, and the third generation still makes the ice cream in a 600-square-foot production room on site.21

Why you should go

Order the malt. Shakes and malts are $7.75 on the shop’s posted menu.1 The Guardian wrote that the milkshakes, made with house ice cream, “taste like you’d expect milkshakes to taste in the movies.”5 On LAist’s AirTalk in July 2026, co-owner John Fosselman suggested a malt, a shake or a hot fudge sundae.4

Then taste your way down the case. The shop scoops from 48 flavors, and LAist says you can sample all you want or ask the clerks for their favorites.14 The menu runs from Rum Raisin, Spumoni and Peppermint Candy to Taro, Lychee, Ube and Macapuno, which it describes as young coconut “straight out of Manilla.”1 LAist called the macapuno a favorite.3 Around Alhambra lists seasonal flavors such as Irish Coffee, Sweet Corn and Black Licorice.7 A single scoop is $4.50, a double $6.50, a triple $7.75. A banana split is $9.50 and a root beer float $6.15.1

Go after dinner if you like. Fosselman’s is open 10 a.m. to 10 p.m. Monday through Saturday and 11 a.m. to 10 p.m. on Sunday.14 You can sit down or take it to go.1 The LA Times described a tiny dining room with a few mauve banquettes, black-and-white photos from Iowa and Pasadena on the walls, and glass cases holding a 1903 ice cream scoop, a recipe book from Waverly Bottling Works, 1920s milk bottles and L.A. County Fair medals from the 1940s.2 There is also a candy counter with more than 50 kinds of old-fashioned candy by the pound.1 Ice cream cakes need an advance order: by 4 p.m. the day before for Monday through Thursday, and by 4 p.m. Thursday for Friday through Sunday.1

What makes it different. The menu says all the ice cream is made on the premises in small batch freezers.1 John told LAist in 2019 that it is “super premium,” 16% butterfat with low overrun, meaning less air than most ice cream, and that it has no preservatives.3 If the flavor you want isn’t in the case, order 12 gallons and they will make a batch of it.27 “Once we did some lavender cookie thing; it was for 7-year-olds,” John told the LA Times.2 He is blunt about the location: “We’re a destination. There’s no reason to come here otherwise, unless you’re going to Costco or Target.”2

Beer, then soda, then ice cream

It starts with a brewery. Peter Fosselman built one in Waverly, Iowa, in 1883, according to a centennial family history by his great-granddaughter Julie Schnieders in The South Pasadenan. When Iowa’s prohibition laws arrived, it became Waverly Bottling Works and made soda.6 His son, Christian Anthony Fosselman, came across a mechanized ice cream machine at a bottlers convention. The accounts differ on where: the LA Times says Los Angeles, LAist says California, and Schnieders writes Toronto.236 He bought one, and in 1919 he founded Fosselman’s in Waverly.6 Before refrigeration, Schnieders writes, he cut ice from the frozen river in winter and kept it in ice houses to hold the ice cream through summer.6

Southern California offered something Iowa couldn’t: a year-round ice cream season.2 LAist’s 2019 history dates the family’s move to Pasadena to 1921. Schnieders, LAist’s 2026 interview and The Guardian say 1924.3645 Fosselman’s Creamery delivered milk, butter, cream and ice cream around the valley.6 The first shop opened in 1937, in South Pasadena according to the LA Times and Schnieders, in Pasadena according to LAist.263 Christian’s son Bill met his future wife, Mary, when she started waitressing there in 1939 for 25 cents an hour. She remembered City Hall setting aside the back booth to discuss city matters.6

In 1941 Christian opened the Alhambra shop and production plant, where all the ice cream is now made.23 His sons Bill, Jim and Bob ran the business after him. The South Pasadena, Highland Park and Glendale shops all closed in the 1970s, leaving Alhambra.26

In 1989, brothers Chris and John Fosselman, then 20 and 23, bought out their uncles, and a few years later their father, Bob, by LAist’s account. Schnieders writes that they bought out all three in 1989.36 They rebuilt the wholesale side, which LAist reported in 2019 served more than 600 clients around Southern California.3 They also added flavors for a changing San Gabriel Valley. “I think our flavors reflect the diversity of L.A.,” John told LAist.3

What stayed the same

The base. The LA Times reported that it has four ingredients (cream, milk, sugar and a vegetable-based stabilizer), mixed for Fosselman’s by Scott Bros. Dairy in Chino from the recipe Christian created in Iowa.2 John put it plainly to LAist:3

“The formula is the exact same as it has been for 100 years. We haven’t changed that at all.”

The volume is less settled. The LA Times reported 1,200 to 2,500 gallons every weekday in 2016; Around Alhambra put it at about 2,000 to 2,500 gallons a week in 2025.27 For the 2019 centennial, the brothers redecorated the shop with archival family photos.3 And John still quotes his father: “Make it so good they can’t resist it.”3

If you go

Fosselman’s is at 1824 W. Main St. in Alhambra (map), open every night until 10. Taste a few flavors, pick one for the road, and order the double-chocolate malt The Guardian told readers to make a beeline for. Follow along at @fosselmansicecreamco.
