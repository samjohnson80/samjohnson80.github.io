Mijares, Pasadena: stone-ground tamales and a $25.75 combo plate from the family that started at a kitchen window in 1920

Pasadena’s oldest Mexican restaurant began with tortillas handed through a window on Fair Oaks. Four generations later it seats 600. Go for the Platillo Mexicano, the tamales, and Sunday brunch.

Before it was a restaurant, it was a window. Around 1918, Jesucita Mijares, who had fled hardship in Mexico, was running a small tortilla factory and boarding house on South Fair Oaks Avenue in Pasadena, selling handmade tortillas and plates of food through her kitchen window. “Neighbors took notice,” Pasadena Weekendr wrote. “Word spread.” 7

That window became Mijares, which the family calls Pasadena’s oldest Mexican restaurant. It opened as a business in 1920. Today the hacienda-style place on Palmetto Drive seats more than 600 people, and it remains in the care of her daughter Alice and three generations of the family. 1510

Why you should go

Order the Platillo Mexicano. It’s the house sampler: a chile relleno and a tamale, plus your pick of a cheese enchilada, a crunchy taco or two taquitos, with rice and beans, for $25.75. No substitutions. 2 When the Los Angeles Times put Mijares on its list of the oldest restaurants in L.A., critic Laurie Ochoa ordered it and singled out “a chile relleno with the kind of fluffy batter that home cooks rarely achieve.” 5

Get the tamales, especially now. The menu says the tamales and salsas are made with masa ground on a volcanic stone molino, and tamales run $12.50 each: red pork, chicken and vegetable, or green chile and cheese. 12 On the restaurant’s 2026 calendar, tamale season began November 2. 4 Jesucita’s mole was “especially prized,” the Times wrote; it shows up today in the Mole Enchiladas Puebla ($27.95). 52

Pick your moment. Sundays from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. is Alice’s Mimosa Brunch, with handmade tortillas, a menudo and pozole bar, a street-taco bar and churros. 3 Tuesday to Thursday from 3 to 6 p.m. is happy hour in the cantina, where street tacos are $4 and the Mijares Margarita is $8.50. It’s served in the bar area only. 3 Or ask for George’s Famous Cadillac Margarita, which the family credits to its mixologist, George Palma. 6 The restaurant is closed Mondays. Parking is free on site. 1

What to expect. This is the classic California Mexican combo plate, from a cook the Times calls one of the “underappreciated pioneers” of the style. Ochoa admits she once dismissed this cooking as “Blando-Mex,” then called her last plate “perfect Mexican comfort food.” 5 The room is built for celebrations. Locals call it “Pasadena’s living room,” and families fill the long tables for birthdays and quinceañeras. 7 Bring friends, but know the house rules: no separate checks, and gratuity is added for large parties and reservations. 2

The backstory: a window, a fire, and her house

Jesucita came north from Jalisco, fleeing the Mexican Revolution. 51 She ground her masa on a metate she had carried from Mexico, and switched to a stone grinder as demand grew. 85 Her great-granddaughter Mary Recendez put it simply:

“She just really found a passion in feeding people and connecting with them through food.”

Old customers still bring Mary stories of how Jesucita handed out candy to neighborhood kids, and how families could bring their own beer in paper bags. 7

The sources don’t agree on when the business reached Palmetto Drive. Pasadena Weekendr said 1940, the Los Angeles Times said 1949, and an ABC7 report said it had been at the Palmetto location for more than a century. 859 What the family agrees on is the land: “Our grandmother purchased the property here, which were four houses,” Tom Recendez told ABC7. The largest became the restaurant. 9

In 1979 an arson fire destroyed it. 79 Alice, who told ABC7 at 91, “I was a waitress for 30 years, I was a busboy, I was everything,” refused to quit. 9 She opened a second restaurant on Washington Boulevard to keep out-of-work employees going, reopened in Altadena in 1980, and brought Mijares back to Pasadena in 1984. 810 Jesucita died in 1988. 1

Her home was on the property, and in 2002 the family rebuilt it as the banquet hall. 7 Mary, who was raised in that house, tells guests: “I always tell customers, when you celebrate here, you’re celebrating in her home.” 7 In 2005 Congressman Adam Schiff honored the family on the House floor for the restaurant’s 85th anniversary. 8 In September 2026 the family marked 106 years with its annual tequila tasting. 10

If you go

Mijares is at 145 Palmetto Dr. in Pasadena, at Pasadena Avenue. Come any day but Monday. Order the Platillo Mexicano and a tamale while the season’s on, or make it a Sunday and let brunch do the work. Jesucita’s motto is still the house rule: “Mi casa es su casa.” 1
