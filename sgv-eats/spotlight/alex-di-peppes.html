Alex Di Peppe’s

A third-generation Arcadia Italian dinner house with hand-tossed pizza, red-sauce pasta and customers who once asked the owner to perform their wedding. What to order, when to go, and the story.

Two customers first walked into Alex Di Peppe’s when they were little children. They grew up, got married, and invited the restaurant’s owner, Larry DiPeppe, to perform the wedding.4 Larry shared it for the Los Angeles Conservancy’s 2024 Legacy Business profile of his family’s Arcadia Italian restaurant, as one of the sweet parts of the job.4

That is the kind of place this is: a family dinner house that has been in the same family since 1969, with a menu of pizza, red-sauce pasta and parmigiana.

Why you should go

You want a plate of red-sauce Italian and a pizza on the same table. The family describes “large portions of home style cooked Italian cuisine.”1 The Conservancy puts the specialty as Southern Italian food “with extra red sauce and pizza.”4

Start with the pasta. The menu’s specialty pastas are ravioli (meat or cheese), baked penne smothered in mozzarella, lasagna (beef or spinach), gnocchi and fettuccine Alfredo. There is also a Pasta Sampler: half an order of lasagna plus your choice of two others from spaghetti, penne, angel hair, gnocchi or ravioli.2 Pasta dishes come with bread and butter or garlic bread, and for $2.25 more you can make it a complete dinner with a cup of soup or a salad.2 A third-party menu listing updated in November 2025 shows ravioli and gnocchi at $14.50, lasagna at $15.95, the sampler at $17.50, eggplant parmigiana at $18.50 and chicken parmigiana at $20.50, so expect something near those numbers, and expect prices to move.6

Reviewers don’t agree on the lasagna. Restaurantji’s summary of reviews singles out the fettuccine Alfredo, eggplant parm and chicken parmigiana, notes that “portions are generous” and that garlic bread comes free, and adds: “While the lasagna may not be a favorite, the ravioli and gnocchi are exceptional.”7 The sampler is a low-risk way to form your own opinion.

Then the pizza. The pizzas are “hand tossed, New York style, with a medium thick crust,” made with Wisconsin mozzarella, and the dough is “made fresh daily.” They come in 8-, 10-, 12- and 16-inch sizes.2 Two house pies carry family names: Alex’s Special (pepperoni, sausage, salami, meatballs, olive, garlic, mushroom, onions and green peppers, or pick up to six items) and Larry’s Special (ground beef, onion, green pepper and fresh tomato).2 If you’re feeding a table, the menu lists family specials: a small one-topping pizza with spaghetti and salad “feeds 2–3.”2

When to go. This is a dinner spot: Wednesday through Sunday, 4:00 to 8:15 p.m. Closed Monday and Tuesday.3 The menu page also mentions a Tuesday-through-Thursday, dine-in-only pasta-and-meatballs special, though the hours page lists Tuesday as closed, so ask when you arrive.23 No holiday hours are posted on the pages we found, so check with the restaurant before a Thanksgiving-week visit.

What to expect. LA Beat’s Nikki Kreuzer described the current space as a strip-mall restaurant that is “comfortably family-style with walls trimmed in brick, wooden tables and booths.”5 It is a small operation: the Conservancy counted two full-time and seven part-time employees.4

From South San Gabriel to Live Oak Avenue

Alessandro and Elsa DiPeppe came from Italy in the early 1950s. He found work as a baker and she worked in a macaroni factory. In 1958 Elsa left her job to start a family, and in 1969, the restaurant’s own history says, “Alex and Elsa took a leap of faith.” They opened their first restaurant in South San Gabriel, serving classic Southern Italian cooking from family recipes.1

In 1978 they relocated the restaurant to Arcadia, “where it has been ever since,” and their son Larry took it over in 1985.1 The restaurant’s website says the grandchildren have also contributed, and calls the business third-generation.1 A directory listing from the Las Tunas Drive years credits Larry and his wife, Jackie, with carrying the family recipes forward and says Larry makes everything fresh daily, “the real secret to the pizza dough.”8

The sources tell the dates a little differently. LA Beat dated the Arcadia restaurant to 1978 on Las Tunas Drive and said it moved about a mile to its present spot in 2015.5 The family’s own account starts in 1969 in South San Gabriel and does not mention the Live Oak move. Both agree on the 1978 Arcadia years and on the family running it throughout. The restaurant’s website prints 608 E. Live Oak Ave. for the address, while the Conservancy and listing sites say 610.34

The Conservancy’s profile is candid about what keeps a place like this going. The challenges were the COVID pandemic and the difficulty of handing down a family business when the next generation has other interests or a better-paying job elsewhere. Larry said customers supported the restaurant a great deal during COVID.4 The family’s own pledge is simple:

“To this day, we continue to serve Italian food in the same way that Alex and Elsa did.”1

If you go

Alex Di Peppe’s is at 610 E. Live Oak Ave. in Arcadia, open Wednesday through Sunday evenings.3 Bring a group, order a sampler and a pizza to share, and look for Larry’s Special on the pizza list. Here is the map. A restaurant where customers once asked the owner to perform their wedding is one worth meeting.
