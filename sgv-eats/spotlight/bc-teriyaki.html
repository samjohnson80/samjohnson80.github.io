B&C Teriyaki, Monrovia: the son who left fashion to keep his parents’ stand from being sold

Since 1990 on Foothill Boulevard: scratch teriyaki and curry bowls, a service window, and a second generation that refused to let the place go.

In early 2025, SFGate’s Los Angeles food editor Karen Palmer wrote about a 28-year-old in Monrovia who had taken over the family restaurant and was posting honest Instagram reels about how hard the work was.2 The place was B&C Teriyaki, a stand with a service window and a handful of outdoor tables on West Foothill Boulevard. The owner was Ben Yap. Before late 2023, his background was in e-commerce and online fashion. Then his parents nearly sold the stand, and he stepped in instead.2

The hook is simple: the recipes stayed, the systems changed, and January after the takeover became the busiest month on record.2

Why you should go

Order a bowl. The menu is teriyaki and curry bowls of rice, meat, and vegetables, plus plates and a few tacos and burritos.14 On the restaurant’s Cash App ordering menu, a chicken teriyaki bowl is $8.82 and a beef teriyaki bowl is $11.63. Curry bowls match those prices. A combo beef-and-chicken teriyaki is $14.03; the beef plate is $14.25 and the chicken plate $13.44.4 Uber Eats lists the same bowls higher ($11.46 chicken, $13.81 beef), which is common with delivery markup.5 Prefer the Cash App figures for walk-up ordering, and check the board when you arrive.

Ask about the sauces. Both the teriyaki and the curry are made from scratch every morning from John Yap’s recipes.12 Ben told SFGate the teriyaki is different from most: “A lot of restaurants use honey to thicken and sweeten teriyaki, but we lean away from the sweeter side and boil onions and garlic into ours.”2 SFGate describes the curry as more like a tangy hot sauce than a Southeast Asian curry.2 The About page on the restaurant’s site calls it a spicy savory sauce with a mild punch.1

Go on a weekday or Saturday, not Sunday. Public listings agree the stand is closed Sunday and open Monday through Saturday starting at 10 a.m. They disagree on closing time. The restaurant’s Cash App ordering page lists 10 a.m. to 6:45 p.m.4 Google-style and Tripadvisor-style listings often say 10 a.m. to 7 p.m.6 Uber Eats shows a delivery window of 10 a.m. to 6:30 p.m.5 Confirm before you go. Expect a service window, no indoor seating, and only a few outdoor tables.2

What makes it different. For decades John and Aimee ran B&C with only one other employee.2 Ben has built a larger team, moved the stand off cash-only, added online ordering, launched Instagram, and hosted events such as a Chinatown night market — without touching the recipes.2 His next goal, he told SFGate, is to bottle the sauces. Expansion is not the plan: “I want to have one spot, perfected.”2 Dad John still comes in daily.2

Beef, chicken, and a near sale

John and Aimee Yap named the stand after the first English words they learned: beef and chicken.1 In a Voyage LA interview, Ben said his parents were immigrants from Cambodia and China who met working restaurant jobs, then “took everything they had and borrowed everything they could” to open B&C Teriyaki in 1990.3 He grew up in the stand. For years it stayed a small, efficient operation serving affordable bowls — “a rare thing these days,” SFGate wrote.2

The near miss came after Aimee was diagnosed with breast cancer. She recovered, but the family began to consider selling.2 Ben told Voyage LA that in 2023 his mother decided it was time to retire after those health issues, his father could not run the place alone, and a sale was on the table. “The thought of letting go of the business that brought me and my siblings up did not sit right with me,” he said, “so I stopped everything I had going for me and decided to go all in on the restaurant business.”3

The handoff was not smooth. Ben told Voyage LA that he and his father argued constantly for six months — six days a week, twelve hours a day — before things improved, and that hiring at first meant no-shows and last-minute call-offs.3 The systems Ben introduced (cards, online ordering, social, a bigger crew) were new; the sauces were not.2 Since the takeover, SFGate reported, B&C had its busiest month on record in January, which Ben attributed to his social media push.2

“I’ve tried teriyaki in many different places, and I do think ours is different.”

That is Ben, to SFGate, on the onion-and-garlic sauce his father still makes the same way.2

If you go

B&C Teriyaki is at 901 W. Foothill Blvd. in Monrovia (map). Order a chicken or beef bowl, ask for the curry if you want heat, and follow along at @bc.teriyaki. The stand has been on that corner since 1990. The second generation is still perfecting the one spot.
