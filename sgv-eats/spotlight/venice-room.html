The Venice Room

Since 1955, this Monterey Park bar has handed diners a raw steak and pointed them to the grill. How it works, what to order, when to go, and the Calabrian immigrant who started it.

The waitress set down the plate and said, “Enjoy!” On it sat a red, raw New York strip about six inches long and an inch thick, a baked potato in foil, packets of butter and a massive white French roll. That is how Eater LA’s Lucas Kwan Peterson met lunch at the Venice Room in 2015. The big grill on one side of the dining room was not for show. As he put it, “you’re the customer, and you’re also the grill master.”6

Most steakhouses would never let you near the fire. This one has been handing over the tongs in Monterey Park since 1955.15

Why you should go

Because you get to cook. Order a New York strip or a ribeye and you are handed raw-cut beef, a potato and garlic bread, then you grill it yourself on the gas grills in the back.4 The restaurant calls its steaks fresh, hand-cut and USDA Choice, and sets out a prep bar with a wide variety of seasonings and sauces.23 The Infatuation describes a shelf “with every seasoning imaginable and a big pair of metal tongs.”5

Never grilled a steak? The restaurant says to ask your server how to cook it.2 Peterson went with about four minutes a side for his inch-thick strip, and it came out between medium and medium rare.6 Mind the signs. In 2015 one above the grill read “DO NOT ADJUST FLAME ON GRILL,” and in 2025 The Infatuation noted a grill-at-your-own-risk sign posted “for a reason.”56

What to order. The restaurant’s online menu lists an 8-ounce ribeye at lunch for $14.95, and at dinner a 10-ounce New York for $18.95 or a 10-ounce ribeye for $23.95. Salad, baked potato, garlic bread and dessert are a $6 add-on. The dinner special for two comes with a bottle of wine: $61.95 with New Yorks, $71.95 with ribeyes.3 Recent press quotes higher numbers. Eater SF put the lunch special at $17 for steak only or $24 with sides and dessert in April 2026, and LAist put a steak dinner at $25 to $35 and the for-two special with house wine at about $75 to $79 in September 2026.94 Ask before you order.

Or come Wednesday. The Wednesday lunch menu adds a Build-A-Burger and a patty melt, $10.95 each on the site.3 In 2018 L.A. Taco reported that Wednesday regulars appear “like clockwork.”8

Know what you are getting. Reviewers are candid. Peterson called his steak “good: not great, but good,” then said that for the money it was “fan-freaking-tastic.”6 The Infatuation wrote that no one pretends it is the best beef in town, and called a meal there “a fun night out that feels fancy and cheap at the same time.”5

When to go. It opens at noon Monday through Saturday and runs until 11 p.m., or midnight on Friday and Saturday. Sunday is 11 a.m. to 11 p.m. Reservations are not available, and it closes on New Year’s Day, Easter, Memorial Day, the Fourth of July, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas.1 Come on a weekend night for karaoke.24

What to expect. Gondola paintings on the walls, red leatherette booths, bartenders in suit vests and lighting The Infatuation calls “cave-level dim.” The regulars include ex-bikers and retired cops.5

Papa Joe’s place

Joseph Lombardo, an immigrant from Calabria in southern Italy, opened the Venice Room in 1955, before the 60 freeway existed, L.A. Taco reported.8 In a 2016 video, Eater described a dark, cave-like room with faux frescoes and a huge grill in the middle of the dining room.7 Its décor and grill-your-own steak dinners were the same “since the sixties,” a point of pride for the family.8

“Papa” Joe kept coming in. In 2015, well into his 90s, he was there about five nights a week, in the same seat at the bar, chatting with customers.6 The room collected stories. Elena Ornelas, who started coming in after high school in the early 1980s, told L.A. Taco that cops and bikers mixed there as long as no one wore uniforms or started trouble, and that the detectives on one infamous 1980s case used it as a meeting spot:8

“The detectives who investigated the Night Stalker in the 80s would meet here at the V-Room to discuss the case.”

What stayed

Papa Joe died in 2017 at 98. In 2018 his sons, Sal and Dom Lombardo, sold the bar to a company named on the public notice as “Da’ Crunch, Inc.”8 “I’m tired,” Sal told L.A. Taco. “I’m 70 years old.” Regulars worried the place would change. A server said the new owners wanted to keep its traditions.8

Seven years later, the reviews read like the old ones: the raw steak, the grill, the booths, the karaoke.45 In April 2026, Eater SF listed it among the California restaurants still keeping the grill-your-own steakhouse going, a format that emerged in the midcentury.9 LAist’s take in September: “heaven on earth.”4

If you go

The Venice Room is at 2428 S. Garfield Ave. in Monterey Park, just off the Pomona Freeway.15 Bring a friend, season your steak at the prep bar, and take the tongs. If it comes out wrong, as The Infatuation puts it, blame yourself.5 Get directions on Google Maps.
