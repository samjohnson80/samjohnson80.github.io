Dai Ho, Temple City: the Taiwanese beef noodle soup people plan their whole day around, three hours at a time

Jim and May Ku opened their first noodle shop in 1987. Today their Temple City room is open from 11:30 to 2:30, six days a week, and it still fills up.

There is a sign over the dining room at Dai Ho. According to LA Weekly, it reads: “No Book & Newspaper Reading, Please.” 4 It isn’t a joke. The room has about a dozen tables, the doors are open for three hours, and Eater LA says the room fills fast and stays full. 41

Jonathan Gold, the Los Angeles Times critic, called Dai Ho “the place everybody loves to hate.” Then he admitted it was where he headed whenever the craving hit. 2 In April 2026, Eater LA put it in a headline: Los Angeles’s best beef noodle soup restaurant, open three hours a day. 1

Why you should go

Order the beef stew soup noodles. This is the bowl people come for. LA Weekly wrote that it is on virtually every table. 4 Eater LA describes tender cubes of beef, chewy noodles, “fistfuls of spinach and shaved green onions,” and a deep broth. 1 Gold’s version was less polite and more convincing: noodles that were “magnificent, slithery, yet chewy and supple, served in a tart, angry-red broth,” with chunks of braised beef “so soft that they collapse at a touch.” 2 A current online menu lists the bowl at $20. 7 Gold admitted it cost a buck or two more than other bowls nearby, and added: “Sometimes quality costs.” 2

Then build the table. For a noodle without soup, get the sesame dry noodles ($16 on the same listing), which Eater LA says come “completely coated in a glossy sesame sauce.” 17 Time Out’s critic said they could convert people who don’t usually like dan dan noodles, and also recommends the pork bean dry noodles. 6 Up front there’s a cold case of side dishes you grab yourself: soy sauce eggs, spicy cucumbers, beef tripe, shredded tofu. 126 The Michelin Guide also singles out the beef tripe and pork shank. 5

Pro tip. Eater LA says any noodle can be made spicy, even though the menu doesn’t say so. Just ask. 1

When to go. Tuesday through Sunday, 11:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. It’s closed Mondays. 51 The Michelin Guide says lines form long before the doors open, and Eater LA says the room fills fast and stays full. Early is your friend. 51

What to expect. Order at the counter, under a short menu. Bring cash. You may share a table with strangers. The food comes fast, “lingering is strongly discouraged,” and Eater LA says you’ll be in and out in about 20 minutes. 154

The backstory: “everybody gets along”

Jim and May Ku came from Taiwan in the mid-1980s. In 1987 they opened a noodle shop in Alhambra called My Kitchen. May had taught herself to cook in Taiwan, making her own soup and noodle recipes for friends. As their daughter Tina told Food GPS in 2007, “Those dishes were what my mom knew how to do. It’s traditional Chinese fast food.” 3

Eleven years later, they moved to Temple City. They kept the same Chinese characters on the sign but took a new English name, Dai Ho, which Food GPS reported means “everybody gets along.” 3

The move caused a small mystery. The new owners of the Alhambra space kept the old Chinese characters too, and regulars assumed it was the same kitchen. One customer carried duck wings from Alhambra to Temple City to ask May why the food had gotten so bad. She pointed out that she hadn’t made them. Her duck wings had two joints. Theirs had three. Word got around. 3

The shop has always kept a short menu. Food GPS counted eight dishes on the official menu in 2007, plus May’s cold dishes, which some customers froze and carried on flights to Taiwan, Beijing and Hawaii. 3 Hours have gotten shorter over time. In 2007 the dining room was open 21 hours a week, and Tina said her parents “are tired and don’t need that much anymore.” Tina had trained at a culinary school in Pasadena and learned her mother’s recipes: “I’ve pretty much got everything down.” 3 By 2012, Gold noted a 3 p.m. close. 2 Today it’s 2:30. 5

The rules have relaxed a little too. In 2012 Gold wrote that the kitchen refused to alter anything. 2 LA Weekly said in 2018 that Jim Ku had a long reputation for turning down an order he didn’t want to serve. 4 By 2026, Eater LA reported you can ask for any noodle spicy. 1 The no-reading sign is still up. 1

If you go

Dai Ho is at 9148 Las Tunas Drive in Temple City. Pick a weekday, show up before 11:30 with cash, and order the beef noodle soup with a soy egg on the side. Leave the book in the car. You’ll be back on the road in 20 minutes, and you won’t need to read anything.
