Lucky Boy, Pasadena: the three-egg breakfast burrito that wasn’t on the original menu, served from 5 a.m. to midnight on Arroyo Parkway

Two Greek immigrants who had never worked in a restaurant bought a burger stand out of the newspaper in 1961. A decade or so later, a customer asked for his breakfast in a tortilla. Go for that burrito, get it with sausage, and stay for a burger.

The breakfast burrito at Lucky Boy starts the same way every time. Three beaten eggs hit the flattop the moment the order comes in, next to cubes of russet potato that have been crisping at the edges in a long, low run on the griddle. Then come the meat, a handful of shredded cheddar, and a flour tortilla that gets its own turn on the grill for the marks. 1 Eater LA once put it in a headline as “LA’s Most Popular Breakfast Burrito.” 1

Here is the strange part. “The breakfast burrito wasn’t even on the original menu,” owner Christina Karagias told Eater. 1

Why you should go

Order the breakfast burrito. It’s the reason people know the name. The L.A. Times called Lucky Boy “a decades-old Pasadena institution known for its monster breakfast burritos” and “a restaurant that comes up during any serious conversation regarding the genre.” Its verdict: “It’s nothing fancy, but it’s consistent,” and the ratios “make for a first-rate burrito.” 2 LAist put it on its list of L.A.’s 25 essential breakfast burritos and called it “large enough to be split into anywhere from two to four meals.” 3 Eater counted roughly 250 sold a day back in 2017. 1

Get it with sausage. The bacon is fried, not griddled, so Eater’s advice was that classicists should opt for the sausage. Chorizo is popular too, but Eater reported that it usually runs out in the morning, so go early if that’s your pick. 1 The menu also has a carne asada breakfast burrito, a vegetarian one, a vegan one and a gluten-free one. 4

Then remember it’s a burger stand. “These days, it’s all about the burgers, the chili cheese fries, and the breakfast burritos,” Eater wrote. 1 The menu runs from a quarter-pound cheeseburger to a triple, a pastrami burger, and a Western Burger with an onion ring inside. Wash it down with an Orange Bang, a horchata coffee ($3.75) or a shake (from $4.10). The menu even invites you to make your own entree, online or in person. 4

Go when it suits you. Breakfast burritos are served all day and night. The Arroyo Parkway shop opens at 5 a.m. and closes at midnight every day; Walnut Street runs 6:45 a.m. to 10:45 p.m. Both are open 363 days a year and close only on Thanksgiving and Christmas, so plan around Thursday, Nov. 26. 45 Both locations take online orders. 4

Know what you’re walking into. Eater described a walk-up window with some limited indoor seating. 1 Not every critic is a fan. The Infatuation gave it a 6.6, calling the burrito “the size of a newborn” but saying “you end up eating mostly potatoes that are mushy rather than crispy.” 6 The L.A. Times found the opposite balance. Decide for yourself.

The backstory: a newspaper ad and a customer in a hurry

John Rellos and Tasos Karagias immigrated from Greece and had never worked in restaurants. In 1961 they saw a Lucky Boy burger place for sale in the newspaper and bought it. That first stand was in South Gate, and it was modeled on a small Texas chain from the 1950s. The partners soon added a second location in Pasadena, then two more by 1975. 7

The burrito came later. According to the story Eater heard, sometime in the 1970s a customer on the go asked for his breakfast “all rolled up into one big handful.” 1 The menu shifted with Southern California: tacos and burritos took over, and almost nobody orders the old Greek diner staples like gyros anymore. 1 (The gyro sandwich and a gyro quesadilla are still on the menu. 4)

Christina Karagias watched her father and uncle build Lucky Boy, according to UCLA’s Center for the Study of Hellenic Culture, which notes that she studied at USC’s Marshall School of Business and later bought restaurants of her own. 8 Tasos Karagias died in 2015. John Rellos kept visiting the Pasadena restaurant regularly until he died in July 2023 at 98. 7

The place still acts like a neighbor. Hey SoCal reported that during the Eaton Canyon fire, Karagias fed fire departments, police and fire victims around the clock, and named Lucky Boy its business of the week in November 2025. 9

If you go

Lucky Boy is at 640 S. Arroyo Pkwy. and 531 E. Walnut St. in Pasadena. Order the breakfast burrito with sausage, at 5 a.m. or 11 p.m., and add a Western Burger if you’re sharing. Sixty-five years after two partners answered a newspaper ad, the window is still open.
