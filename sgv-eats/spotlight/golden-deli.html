Golden Deli, San Gabriel: the cha gio, the pho, and a closed Wednesday

A Vietnamese noodle shop at 815 W. Las Tunas Dr. since 1981. What the menu lists now, what the critics could not agree on, and why Wednesday is still the wrong day.

Laurie Ochoa and Jonathan Gold, in the Los Angeles Times on March 12, 1992, started at the door. “Saturday afternoons, the line spills out the door of the Golden Deli, the Vietnamese noodle shop where regulars know the pho dac biet is worth the 15-minute wait for a table.” Vietnamese pop was playing, “possibly an English-Viet version of The Eagles’ ‘Lyin’ Eyes.’” The address is still 815 W. Las Tunas Drive, in a San Gabriel mini-mall.1

Why you should go

Order the cha gio, then build the pho. Fried pork egg rolls, phan cha gio, are $15.50: five with a vegetable plate, or six without.2 On November 11, 1993, Gold called them “crusty, golden things, four inches long and as thick as a fat man’s thumb, five to an order,” and wrote, “These spring rolls are possibly the greatest on the planet.”3 Eight months earlier he and Ochoa wrote that they “could be crisper.”4 On November 10, 2011, he wrote the sentence quoted below. The about page still gives that “observable universe” praise to an unnamed critic.56 The Infatuation, on May 27, 2025, wrote, “Plain and simple, Golden Deli’s chả giò is the best we’ve had.”7

The about page still describes pho dac biet as rice noodle soup with ribeye, brisket, tendon, and tripe.6 The ordering menu does not use that name. Beef pho starts at $14.50. A large bowl is extra noodles, not extra meat. Ribeye (tai), brisket (nam), tendon (gan), and tripe (sach) are $4 add-ons.2 In 1992 the dac biet was “Hearty, gamy, chewy and shot through with sweet spice.”4 In 2007 Gold wrote of a “particularly complex, cinnamon-scented broth.”8 In 2025 the Infatuation found a broth “so light it’s nearly translucent,” and, on the rare-steak bowl, “a little kiss of cinnamon and star anise.”7 The descriptions do not match.

Com suon is two pork chops with rice, $14.75. Phan chao tom is $18.50: “2 shrimp paste with rice vermicelli, lettuce, mint, and rice paper.” The menu does not mention sugar cane.2 The about page does.6 Gold, in 1993, described “pounded shrimp paste molded around a piece of sugar cane and grilled.”3 Cathy Chaplin, in 2009, said a vermicelli bowl would have been better “if the shrimp paste had been served with its traditional sugarcane skewer.”9 Soda chanh and ca phe sua da are $5.50 each. Che ba mau, the three-color dessert the about page calls 3 mau, is $6.50.26 No beer or wine is on the drink list. Gold’s 2011 listing said “No alcohol.”5

Go Thursday through Tuesday. On October 28, 2026, the restaurant’s site listed San Gabriel from 10:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. those days, closed Wednesday, with pickup and delivery until 7:45 p.m. San Gabriel is listed for dine-in and takeout.10 Eater LA lists the same dining-room hours and, on this Wednesday, “Today: Closed.”11 Yelp’s listing shows the same week and does not mark the place permanently closed.12 Gold’s 1993 review already said closed Wednesday, and the hours then ran later, from 9 or 9:30 a.m. to as late as 10 p.m.3 Nguyen, in L.A. TACO on January 21, 2016, wrote of people outside “cursing themselves, as you soon will be, for forgetting that the restaurant is closed on Wednesdays.”13 The 2011 listing names every day but Wednesday, then prints “Closed August.”5 The site’s week now names Wednesday as the closed day.10

The Infatuation in 2025 still found “lines trailing out the front door” six days a week, and wrote that on a weekend you might spend as long in line as at the table. Temple City waits, it said, are “usually half as long.”7 That shop, at 9664 Las Tunas Dr., is closed Monday and open 11:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. otherwise, Wednesday included.10 In 2011 Gold had San Gabriel waits “up to an hour in the parking lot.”5

Who opened it

The about page says the founders came from Vietnam, arrived in Los Angeles in 1975, and married in 1976 at Mission San Gabriel Arcángel, “the mission’s first couple to wed in a traditional Vietnamese ceremony.” Golden Deli opened in 1981 as Chinese fast food, added Vietnamese dishes on weekends, and “within about two years” served Vietnamese food only. The page does not name the founders.6 Gold, on December 19, 2007, wrote that the Le family ran it and that Michael Le’s parents founded it. He had seen Michael there since Michael was in high school. That column also had Vietnam House across the street and Saigon Flavor by the mall.8 The site now lists San Gabriel and Temple City, not those names. The Infatuation in 2025 still called this one family-run.710

Nguyen, in 2016, reported the 1980 census: Asians less than 10 percent of San Gabriel, white residents about 52 percent, and “at last count” nearly 61 percent Asian. The count is from 2016.13 Gold, in 2011, asked how it lasted “nearly 25 years in a neighborhood where some of the restaurant spaces turn over twice a year.”5 In 1992 he and Ochoa had it in Las Tunas Plaza, the lot “almost always filled to capacity,” beside Indonesian and Taiwanese restaurants.1

This is Jonathan Gold, in LA Weekly on November 10, 2011.

“Because Golden Deli has the best cha gio — fried Vietnamese spring rolls — in the observable universe, as its many Caltech regulars may tell you, and its fans will do anything for a crack at the burnished, bubbly, deep-fried cylinders.”

If you go

815 W. Las Tunas Dr., San Gabriel. Skip Wednesday. Order the egg rolls. If you want the pho the about page describes, add the ribeye, the brisket, the tendon, and the tripe. Leave time for the line the reviews keep describing. The map is here.210
