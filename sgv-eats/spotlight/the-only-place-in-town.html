The Only Place in Town

It opened in 1945 as The Headliner. Then Sierra Madre gave it the name it has now. Eighty-one years on, it serves breakfast, lunch and dinner on Sierra Madre Boulevard, with a Bourbon Burger, banana bread French toast and ice cream made in house. What to order, when to go, and the story.

In 2012, windstorms tore through Sierra Madre and the power went out. The restaurant at 110 W. Sierra Madre Blvd. got hold of a studio generator and kept going. For almost a week, co-owner William Kefalas told Voyage LA, it was one of the only businesses open in the city, and people with no power came in for food, for a way to cook, or to charge their phones.1 For a few days, the name on the sign was close to literal.

The name is the story here. The place opened in 1945 as The Headliner.2 Kefalas says the original family rebranded it in the 1990s by asking the people of the city what to call it. “They voted on ‘The Only Place in Town’ as at the time it was.”1 The restaurant’s own site says simply that the residents of Sierra Madre renamed it.2 Other tellings make it a nickname. OpenTable’s listing says locals called it the only place in town because there was no other restaurant around, and the name was changed later.6 Food writer Peter Dills put it this way in Mountain Views News in August: “Somewhere along the way people started calling it ‘the only place in town,’ and the nickname stuck.”5 Vote or nickname, the town picked it.

Why you should go

One menu for the whole day. ABC7 summed up the range as everything “from big juicy bourbon sauced burgers, to banana French toast,” plus salads, short rib tacos “and pretty much anything you can think of.”4 Kefalas calls it “a place for elevated comfort food, unique dishes and high quality ingredients.”1

What to order. From the May 2026 menu on the restaurant’s site:3 the Bourbon Burger, a half-pound Angus patty with applewood-smoked bacon, grilled onions, tomato, Havarti, Thousand Island and bourbon-chipotle sauce, $22. Before 2 p.m., the banana bread French toast: panko-breaded banana bread with mascarpone crème, fresh berries and bacon or sausage, $19. The Nicosia avocado toast stacks grilled halloumi and cherry tomatoes on ciabatta under a Kalimyrna fig-balsamic reduction, wildflower honey and basil, $19. At dinner, the bourbon-chipotle pork chop is $27. One DoorDash reviewer who ordered it twice wrote, “The only place in town is now my go to when I want pork chops.”9

Save room. The ice cream is made in house: Oreo, strawberry, chocolate-chocolate-chip, caramel pecan, cappuccino and roasted coconut, $3.50 a scoop or $6 for a double. A milkshake made with it is $7. The $9 sundae comes with hot fudge and whipped cream over a house flavor or Fosselman’s vanilla bean,3 from the Alhambra ice cream maker SGV Eats featured earlier. Then take a carrot-zucchini loaf home for $9.50.3 A DoorDash customer who added one “as an afterthought” gave it a one-word review: “Incredible.”9

When to go. Monday through Saturday, 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. Sunday, 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.27 Breakfast runs until 2 p.m.3 Mondays bring short rib tacos at $5.25 each. On Fridays and Saturdays after 4 p.m., a half rack of baby back ribs is $30.3 ABC7 suggested catching the sunset on the patio with a charcuterie board and wine, and noted the boards are sold to go, too.4 The board is $35 on the menu.3

What to expect. A family-owned American restaurant, with a patio the owners expanded during Covid.1 Parties of six or more get an 18% gratuity added.3 Reviews are split. Restaurantji shows 4.4 stars from 271 ratings.7 Yelp, where it is listed as Barney’s Only Place In Town, shows 3.6 from 479 reviews.8 On DoorDash, one customer found the Grandview and Bourbon burgers “just okay,” while another wrote that her husband had the gyro sandwich “and raved about it.”9

Two brothers, second owners

William and Michael Kefalas grew up putting on Greek food festivals for their church in Sierra Madre. Their first business together was Spin Laundry Systems, which put coin-operated laundry and vending machines in San Gabriel Valley apartment buildings. In college both waited tables, Michael at Coco’s in Long Beach and William at Cameron’s Seafood in Pasadena, where he became a manager.1

In 1998 they sold the laundry business and bought Barney’s Ltd in Pasadena, which had been open since 1979. They ran it for 14 years. In 2005 they bought The Only Place in Town. “We purchased it in 2005 as the second owners,” William Kefalas told Voyage LA.1

Since then they have remodeled, changed menus, and added corporate and party catering. After Barney’s closed, they started a school hot lunch program that now serves six schools from the restaurant’s kitchen. During Covid they remodeled and expanded the patio and started delivery.1

The Eaton Fire came close. In March 2025, Michael Kefalas told ABC7 that the restaurant’s kitchen manager and a busboy had lost their homes, and that the restaurant had worked with an anonymous donor to give free meals to others who were displaced. “Sierra Madre is a great town, small town, everybody kind of comes together,” he said.4

William Kefalas’s advice for new owners, in the same Voyage LA interview, reads like the house rule: “Your customers don’t owe you anything, but you owe them everything.”1 Dills, for his part, marveled at the staying power: “Eighty-plus years later, we’re still talking about the place.”5

If you go

The Only Place in Town is at 110 W. Sierra Madre Blvd., in the heart of town.24 Come for banana bread French toast before 2, a Bourbon Burger on the patio at sunset, or a scoop of the house ice cream on the way home. While you’re there, ask which version of the name story they tell. Get directions on Google Maps.
