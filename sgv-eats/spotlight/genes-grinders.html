Gene’s Grinders, Duarte: $14.50 pastrami dips on French rolls baked from scratch every morning, from the family that lost its Monrovia lease after 49 years

In January the Barmaxis family got 51 days to leave the corner of Myrtle and Walnut. Then a detour led them to a storefront a mile east. Go for the pastrami, ask for the top dipped, and look for Andy behind the counter.

The San Gabriel Valley Tribune caught the routine in February: Andreas “Andy” Barmaxis, 70, unlocking the doors of Gene’s Grinders at 1:30 a.m. to bake 500 to 600 French rolls, “never measuring anything.” 1 He had been doing it in Monrovia for 49 years. Days later the shop at the corner of Myrtle and Walnut would be gone, because the landlord had decided not to renew the lease. 14

Gene’s didn’t stay gone. On April 18 the family reopened about a mile east, at 1012 E. Huntington Drive in Duarte. “We didn’t close, we relocated,” Andy’s son Michael told the Tribune. “Same Gene’s.” 3

Why you should go

Order the pastrami. The Tribune describes Gene’s as “famous for its French roll and pastrami sandwiches.” 3 The Pastrami Dip comes with mustard and pickles only, from $14.50. The Pastrami Grinder adds cheese, lettuce, tomato and dressing, from $14.75. 5 For the family’s own version, take a cue from Michael’s wife, Meagan, who will only let her father-in-law make her pastrami one way: “with pickles, mustard and the top dipped in gravy sauce.” 1 Monrovia city manager Dylan Feik told the paper the pastrami is his “go-to.” 2

Or go cold. Gene’s Special stacks ham, salami, capicola, turkey, American and provolone with lettuce, tomato and olive oil, from $13.25. 5 Duarte Mayor Tera Martin Del Campo, a lifelong resident, says she used to drive to Myrtle Avenue for “a turkey sandwich with extra pickles.” 2 The Meatball Special (cheese, lettuce, tomato and marinara) is $13.25. 5 Mustard, mayo, vinegar, pickles, onion and chili flakes are available on request on any grinder. 6

Check the day. There is a hot special each weekday: roast pork on Monday, chicken breast Tuesday and Wednesday, beef steak Milanesa on Thursday, Italian sausage on Friday. 5 Add fries for $3.25. 5

What to expect. It’s an order-at-the-counter sandwich shop in the same plaza as Target, next to Crumbl Cookies. 3 The bread is the difference. Gene’s says Andy bakes the French roll on site, from scratch, every morning, and that he is behind the deli counter six days a week. 4 You can order ahead online. 5 Michael’s advice for the new shop: “Pull up early. Pull up hungry.” 3

The backstory: Gene, “The Greek,” and a detour

There really was a Gene. According to the Tribune, the Barmaxis family bought the shop from Bruce Bruscia, the son of the Gene on the sign. It first stood on Huntington Drive, on Route 66, across the street from Miller Ford. 1 The shop dates itself to 1954 and calls itself “family owned and operated since 1954.” 4 The Tribune gives 1954 as the year of the family’s purchase, though the same story says Andy came to California at 16, so read that date as the start of Gene’s rather than a settled sale date. 1

Andy was born in Corinth, Greece. His father, George, was a machinist in El Monte, and Andy says he learned the value of hard work from his parents. In 1977 the city helped the business move to South Myrtle Avenue, and Andy took over. 1 Regulars call him “The Greek.” Photos in the old shop showed him in the kitchen and at local racetracks. 1 Gene’s says he “brought his love and appreciation of horse racing to the deli counter.” 4 He and his wife, Violet, raised Michael and his sister Anastasia around the shop. 1

Monrovia made it a habit. The Tribune reported that people packed Gene’s in coolers for fishing trips from the 1950s on. Firefighters from the nearby station were regulars, and so were car-show crowds and families after Saturday baseball. 1 When the family announced the closing on Jan. 26, demand tripled their bread production from 500 loaves to 1,500. “We can’t make enough bread, it’s been crazy busy,” Michael said. 1 On the last day, Feb. 18, a line ran out the door half an hour after the 10 a.m. opening. Andy baked from 11 p.m. to 9 a.m., then kept going. Five batches, a record. 2

Then came the detour. Violet couldn’t bear to drive past the old shop, so Michael and Megan took her a different way, past a For Lease sign at the Target center in Duarte. The space wasn’t even on the market yet. “It was perfect!” Michael said. “That spot was for us.” 2 The family said it wasn’t changing the menu, and the crew came along: George Padilla, the longtime manager, plus Christian, Juanito, Mauricio, Pancho and Terry. 2

If you go

Gene’s Grinders is at 1012 E. Huntington Dr. in Duarte, open every day. Order a pastrami dip with the top dipped, or the weekday special. The rolls are baked on site every morning. Different city, same Gene’s.
