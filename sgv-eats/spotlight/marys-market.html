Mary’s Market, Sierra Madre: a 104-year-old canyon café where the burger depends on the weather

Painted rocks point the way to a clapboard cottage by a creek that has fed Sierra Madre Canyon since 1922.

The only signs pointing to Mary’s Market are stones. They sit on street corners in the part of Sierra Madre that locals call the Canyon, painted with the café’s name and an arrow.6 Follow them past the mouth of the Mount Wilson Trail and you reach a cottage-sized diner that has stood there since 1922.6 In 2016 an LA Weekly writer found it selling T-shirts that read “MARY’S MARKET — Find Us if You Can.”7

Once you find it, there are rules. The café posts eight of them on its website, starting with “NO WHINING! (Children or Adults)” and ending with the one that decides your lunch: “BURGERS SERVED FROM 10-1:30 TUE-FRI (No burgers if it’s raining or over 100 degrees outside).”1

Why you should go

Order the Benedict first. The café calls its Eggs Benedict its signature,1 and Eggs Benedict is the dish Yelp shows reviewers mentioning most on a listing with 417 reviews and a 4.4-star average.9 On the 2026 menu, the Eggs Benny is two poached eggs and ham on a toasted English muffin with hollandaise, for $13.75.2

If you’re hungrier, the Cowboy Bowl ($14.50) is, in the menu’s words, “Hammy, bacony pinto beans, roasted potatoes and cheese, topped with two poached eggs and fresh salsa.” Mary’s Favorite Burrito ($17) wraps scrambled eggs, bacon, potatoes, those cowboy beans and cheese in a flour tortilla. Can’t choose? The Dan Bell Remix ($18) gets you half orders of any two of the waffle, the polenta, the Cowboy Bowl, the Zero, or any Benny. For lunch there’s Carolyn’s Chicken Salad, with dill, pecans, grapes and Swiss ($13.50), and the Market Burger, a half-pound patty with Swiss and 1000 Island ($15), served Tuesday through Friday from 10 to 1:30, “and not when it’s raining.”2

Go in the morning. Mary’s is closed Monday, open 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Tuesday through Friday, and 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. on weekends.3 You order at the counter, help yourself to cream and sugar, grab cold drinks from the cooler, and sit down; the house rules say they’ll find you.1 The menu adds a 3% fee on non-cash payments and no substitutions except for dietary restrictions.2

Expect to walk a little. The café says it “was built long before cars were a normal thing,” so parking is limited, and it suggests a short stroll through the neighborhood if nothing is open nearby.4 Inside, Eater LA described white walls covered in vintage signs that regulars gave the place, sun through three big south windows, and a fridge of cold drinks and candy left over from its grocery days. A stream runs behind it.6

A grocery, a mother, and a movie

Before the café there was Carter’s Camp, opened in 1908 by Arthur Carter, son of Sierra Madre founder Nathaniel C. Carter, with tent cabins, a dance hall, an open-air cafeteria and a freshwater pool steps from today’s cottage.6 The store opened in 1922 as Spartan’s Meat and Grocery.56

Then came Mary Perkins, an Irish immigrant. The café’s history page and Eater LA say she bought it in 1966;56 Mountain Views News put it at 1964.8 LA Weekly wrote that she first renamed it the Cottage Market, and that neighbors simply called it “Mary’s.”7 Locals came to call her the “Mother of the Canyon,” Eater LA reported, because hippies who walked up for food and beer often left without paying. Perkins told a local paper why she let it go:6

“That’s what you’re supposed to do. When people are hungry, you’re supposed to feed them. Even if they can’t pay.”

The record splits on what came next. LA Weekly says she retired in 1991 and that three years later a new owner, Tom Hammond, renamed the place Mary’s Market.7 Eater LA says she retired in 1992, announcing it on a homemade sign on the door, and that the market sat closed for two years until Sierra Madre resident John Sanford bought it.6 The café’s own page speaks of “a series of past owners.”3 Eater LA also reported that the counter and swivel stools came from the film Drowning Mona, which shot at the market, and that otherwise little had changed in a century.6

The new Mary’s

Jenny Kay and Heather Morrison met in the fall of 2010 while working at Mary’s, the café’s own page says, and spent years cooking and cleaning there before it was “their turn to carry the torch.”3 Eater LA dates their purchase to 2017;6 Mountain Views News described them as owners in 2018.8 “Some of our customers feel like family to us after all these years,” Morrison told Eater LA.6

The last few years tested that. Mountain Views News reported a 2020 pandemic closure, off-and-on closures after the January 2025 Eaton Fire, then 6.75 inches of February rain and the mudslides. It also reported that the owners received a $25,000 grant from a program administered by the Pasadena City College Women Business Center; they were among 50 area women business owners awarded funds to recover from the wildfires.8

If you go

Mary’s Market is at 561 Woodland Dr. in Sierra Madre (map). Go on a weekday morning, look for the painted rocks, order the Benny at the counter, and check the sky before you count on a burger. Follow the house rules, especially the first one.
