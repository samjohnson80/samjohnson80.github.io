Lucky Baldwins

In 1996 this Old Town Pasadena pub was taking in $600 a day against $1,000 in rent. Two British tourism officials turned it into a Belgian beer landmark. What to order, when to go, and how they did it.

The first time Peggy Simonian walked into the Old Town pub she and David Farnworth were about to run, it was empty. “I came here and there wasn’t a soul here,” she told the Los Angeles Business Journal in 2011. “And I said, ‘What are we buying?’” The pub was taking in about $600 a day. The rent needed about $1,000.1

Farnworth was later made an honorary knight by the Belgian Brewers Guild, a Knight of the Mash Staff, for his work promoting Belgian ales.2 Thirty years on, Lucky Baldwins still pours more than 60 beers on tap a few steps from Colorado Boulevard.3

Why you should go

The fish and chips. The menu calls them LBs’ Famous Fish N Chips: Pacific cod dipped in ale batter, with lemon, homemade tartar sauce and a side.4 “I think there’s this stigma around British food that it’s a little bland,” general manager Patsy Sutton told LAist in May 2026. She credits the beer batter, and the pub uses locally sourced Pacific cod instead of Atlantic. LAist listed the full order at $18.5

Then go British. Bangers and mash ($19 in LAist’s report) come with peas and gravy. The pasties are handheld pies, Cornish beef, cheese and onion, or chicken curry, $14 with crisps or fries.45 Say it right: LAist’s tip is PAH-stee, not PAY-stee.5 There is also cottage pie, steak and Guinness mushroom pie and a Scotch egg. On Sundays from noon, an English Sunday roast comes with Yorkshire pudding, honey-glazed carrots and parsnips.4 The pub’s online menu does not list prices, so check before you order.

The beer. The rotating taps lean Belgian and craft, with Guinness, Stella Artois and Boddingtons as permanent pours.3 Pasadena Magazine wrote in 2020 that there was a time when Chimay or Duvel “could only be found flowing from the taps of Lucky Baldwin’s.”6 The pub’s own site says it ranks among the top five Belgian beer bars in the U.S. That is its claim, not ours.7

Breakfast and early football. Old Town serves breakfast from 9 a.m. to noon every day. The Full English comes with eggs, Irish bacon, a banger, grilled tomato, mushroom, breakfast potatoes and baked beans.8 Soccer matches before 11 a.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. on weekends are shown at the Old Town pub only.9 On a big match day, Pasadena Magazine reported, the doors open as early as 6 a.m.6 LAist counted Liverpool, West Ham and Arsenal supporters’ groups among the regulars.5

When to go. Old Town is open daily from 9 a.m. to 1:30 a.m.10 The pub has posted Thanksgiving dinner at Old Town on Nov. 26, opening at noon and serving from 1 p.m. Its Christmas Beer Festival runs Dec. 4 to 27 at both pubs.11

What to expect. A British pub, not an Irish one, whatever Sheldon Cooper said. In a 2010 episode of The Big Bang Theory, he called it “Pasadena’s most popular Irish watering hole,” as the blog IAMNOTASTALKER transcribed it.12 The Infatuation notes beans on toast and crumpets, and that the pub is named after the tycoon Elias Jackson “Lucky” Baldwin.13 Simonian told LAist she likes a sunny day on the Old Town patio with a Belgian beer.5

Two tourism officials and a struggling pub

Farnworth was English. He moved to Los Angeles from South Africa in 1982 to handle marketing for the British Tourist Authority. Simonian transferred from the authority’s London office in 1992, and they became friends. He bought into Lucky Baldwin’s as a minority partner in 1995. The bar moved to Old Town the next year and was soon close to failing. He asked Simonian to become co-owner and help him turn it around.1 Sources describe the start differently. The British Weekly wrote that the pair bought the business from its founders, Gail and Judah Casburn, in 1996.14 LA Weekly in 2013 described Simonian and Farnworth as having founded it that year.15

Both kept their day jobs. At night, Simonian waited tables and Farnworth tended bar.1 LAist reports it took another three years for the pub to attract a real following, spurred by its first Belgian Beer Festival.5

Sir David

Farnworth made regular trips to Belgium to meet brewers and arrange to ship their beer to America.6 “Back in 1998, we were one of the first places to actually feature his beer,” Simonian told LA Weekly, speaking of Jef Versele, the seventh-generation brewmaster at Brouwerij Van Steenberge. In 2013 that brewery made the pubs a house beer, Lucky Baldwins Red, a 7 percent Belgian amber. The first shipment was 96 kegs.15 The Full Pint called Lucky Baldwins “the first Belgian centric beer bar in Southern California.”2

The Sierra Madre pub, the Delirium, opened in 2006, according to the Business Journal. One 2010 account says 2005.112 Around then Farnworth began losing strength in his arms. By 2011 he could not hold a glass or sign his own name.1 He died on Oct. 28, 2011, after a long struggle with ALS.14 Simonian kept the festivals and the traditions going.6 In 2026 she is still the owner-operator.5

If you go

Lucky Baldwins is at 17 S. Raymond Ave. in Old Town Pasadena. The Delirium Pub is at 21 Kersting Court in Sierra Madre.10 Order the fish and chips, ask what Belgian beer is on, and say pastie the right way. If it is a match morning, come early. Get directions on Google Maps.
