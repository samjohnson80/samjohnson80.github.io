Di Pilla’s, Rosemead: a tile setter’s bad knee, a 1967 pizzeria, and Bowties Di Pilla for $19.25

Tonino and Giuseppina Di Pilla opened it while raising three girls. Their daughter Claudia has run it since 1979, and the family’s names are still on the menu.

Tonino Di Pilla was a tile man. Then he had knee surgery and needed a new trade. According to the Rosemead Report, his wife Giuseppina had an aunt and uncle who ran an Italian restaurant in Inglewood, and they suggested the couple try a small pizzeria of their own. 2 They liked the idea. In 1967 they opened Di Pilla’s in Rosemead. A small pizza with one topping cost $1.30. A large was $2.25. 2

Fifty-nine years later, it’s still in the family, and the restaurant’s own site still calls it “A ‘little slice’ of Pacentro right here in Rosemead!” 5

Why you should go

Order the Bowties Di Pilla. It carries the family name, and it’s the crowd favorite. In 2017 the Rosemead Report said it was the most requested party tray: bowtie pasta in an alfredo cream sauce with a touch of marinara, with peas and ham on request. 2 It’s also the most-liked item on the restaurant’s DoorDash page, where it lists at $19.25. 6 A 2013 review on she dines socal said it came with peas and ham in a homemade pink sauce. The same writer asked for extra bread to finish the sauce. 8

Then order off the family tree. The Rosemead Report pointed out that Bruno Ramunno’s name is on several house specialties, and that some dishes are named for his and Claudia’s children, Dante and Daniela. 2 On the current online menu you’ll find Dante’s Special, with mushrooms, spinach, broccoli, red peppers, and sun-dried tomatoes over house pasta, for $24.75. There’s also Spinach Fettuccine Bruno, with sausage, zucchini, and mushrooms, for $25.75, and Scampi alla Daniela over angel hair for $25.95. 7 The menu also lists a Vitello alla Claudia. 5

Don’t skip the bread. The restaurant calls its pizza bread “renowned.” A box of six is listed at $4.50. 57 Entrées come with bread already, and the 2013 review described a basket of warm garlic bread brought to the table. 58 For classics, spaghetti and meatballs is $19.75, lasagna is $20.25, and a large build-your-own pizza is $26.75. 6 Delivery-app prices can run higher than in the restaurant.

Pro tip. The specials page lists two takeout-only deals. On Tuesdays and Wednesdays, a large cheese pizza is $14.95. Tuesday through Thursday, $47.05 gets a medium cheese pizza, spaghetti, salad, and bread, enough for up to four. Both are plus tax, and the page says prices can change. 3 Seafood plates are served only after 4 p.m., and the menu asks you to allow 30 minutes for the seafood risotto. 5

When to go. It’s closed Mondays. Tuesday through Friday it opens at 11 a.m. for lunch. Saturday and Sunday it’s dinner only, from 4 p.m. 3 Closing times aren’t the same on every listing. Yelp and Google show different hours than the restaurant’s site, and the site’s contact page says to call and confirm. 31011 Google’s popular-times data shows Saturday at 5 and 6 p.m. as the busiest hours, with waits of up to 15 minutes. 10

What to expect. In 2015, The LA Beat described the room as “perfectly old school Italian kitsch,” with brick walls, wood paneling, and burgundy leather booths. It also said, “Plastic grapes and grape vines are strung from the ceiling throughout.” 4 A newer listing summary mentions white linens. 10 The Rosemead Report called the portions generous, and the 2013 reviewer got a next-day lunch out of the leftovers. 28 The contact page says the restaurant isn’t taking reservations right now. 3

The backstory: Pacentro to Rosemead

Tonino and Giuseppina arrived in America in 1958 from Pacentro, a small village in Abruzzo, and opened the restaurant with family recipes from home. 1 They were raising three daughters at the time, Claudia, Franca, and Diana, and all three helped out. 12 (Sources spell her name two ways. The restaurant uses Giuseppina, and the Rosemead Report and PBS SoCal use Guiseppina.) 129

Claudia was Miss Rosemead in 1974. That year she took part in city parades and went to grand openings around town. 24 In 1979, after finishing college, where she studied accounting, she took over. She already knew the job. When her parents traveled, once for about three weeks in Italy, she ran the place. Her father didn’t leave right away. She told the Rosemead Report, “My dad would still come and stick around for a couple of years. It was hard to let go of the restaurant.” 2

Her late husband, Bruno Ramunno, helped run it for much of the time from 1979 until his death. He was a cook who added recipes that are still on the menu. 1 As Rosemead’s Asian community grew, he added dishes with garlic and vegetables, plus seafood. He also liked to leave the kitchen to talk soccer and food with customers. 2 The 2017 article said he had died eight years earlier, but no exact date has been published. 2

Under Claudia, the restaurant added a second, larger dining room and began making party trays for schools and businesses. 2 In 2010, Assemblymember Mike Eng chose Di Pilla’s to represent his district at California Small Business Day in Sacramento. 12 The restaurant’s site says Claudia got the award from Governor Schwarzenegger. In the Rosemead Report, she remembers him giving “a little speech at the beginning.” 12 She also offers school discounts, sponsors teams, and donates to local fundraisers. 19

“People from far away will talk to other people and they’ll say, ‘Oh, I know this place.’” Claudia Di Pilla, Rosemead Report, 2017 2

If you go

Di Pilla’s is at 9013 E. Valley Boulevard in Rosemead, near Rosemead Boulevard. Check the hours first, then go hungry. Get the bowties, add a box of pizza bread, and order one dish named for a Di Pilla. Plan to take some home.
