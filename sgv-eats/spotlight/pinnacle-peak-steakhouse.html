Pinnacle Peak Steakhouse, San Dimas: a 20-ounce mesquite T-bone on old Route 66, and a staff that will cut off your tie

Since 1967 this cowboy steakhouse on a hill above Foothill Boulevard has grilled over mesquite charcoal and banned neckties, with scissors. Go for the Cowboy steak, the beans and the apple cobbler.

Partway through dinner in 2015, the writer Sarah McKinley Oakes watched her waitress come back to the table ringing a cowbell and talking about a city slicker in the place. Then the waitress cut off her husband’s tie with big scissors, and “everyone cheered.” 2

That’s the house rule at Pinnacle Peak Steakhouse in San Dimas: no ties allowed. A server who went by the name Geronimo explained it to a student reporter in 1997: “the cowboys didn’t wear ties.” 1 The cut ties go up on the walls and rafters, and by now many carry dates and signatures. 3 You don’t have to give yours up. Guests can take a tie off instead of losing it. 1

Why you should go

Order the Cowboy. It’s a 20-ounce USDA aged Choice T-bone for $38.99, and the restaurant says it has been cooking its steaks, chicken, ribs and seafood “on genuine mesquite charcoal” since 1967. 89 The staff at Lowrider magazine wrote that when they want steak they skip the big chains and come here, and “our go-to dish is the Cowboy.” 7 Smaller appetite? The Cowgirl is a 15-ounce T-bone for $31.99. Feeding a crowd? The Trail Boss is a 28-ounce porterhouse for $52.99. 8

Don’t skip the sides. Every entrée comes with salad, beans and ranch-style bread. 8 The menu describes the cowboy beans as “seasoned with our special recipe developed 40 years ago.” 8 Finish with apple cobbler à la mode ($6.99), which one Los Angeles writer called “insanely good.” 82 At lunch, the Old Wild West ($15.50) is a sliced flat-iron steak sandwich with mushrooms and onions on garlic toast. 8

When to go. Pinnacle Peak serves lunch and dinner Tuesday through Sunday and is closed Monday. 9 One Yelp reviewer warns that “Dinner gets very busy on the weekends (even at 5:30)” and recommends a reservation. The restaurant takes them. 109 The same reviewer’s family has a ritual worth borrowing: “We always make a stop at the goodwill for ties beforehand.” 10

What to expect. Long wood tables, red-and-white checked tablecloths, and pictures and quotes from the old West on the walls. 3 One visitor found the menus printed on paper bags and a big open fire grill in the kitchen. 2 Yelp lists it as casual and good for groups and kids. Not everyone loves every steak: Pinnacle Peak holds 3.8 stars across more than 1,100 Yelp reviews. 9 In 1997 the Campus Times called the look “yard-sale-meets-dude-ranch,” and meant it kindly. 1

The backstory: a stagecoach stop, a Tucson idea, and a ceiling that filled up

The hill came before the steakhouse. Route 66 Times and the Glendora Public Library both describe the site as an old Wells Fargo stagecoach stop. 43 The Campus Times reported in 1997 that it was built around the turn of the century as the San Dimas Hills Inn, closed during the Depression, and later reopened as a chuck wagon. 1

The idea came from Arizona. The first Pinnacle Peak opened in Tucson in 1962, at Trail Dust Town, a former western movie set. It billed itself as the “Home of the Cowboy Steak” and did well enough to expand to California, with restaurants in San Dimas, Colton and Santee. 35 The San Dimas building went up in 1967 in a Spanish modern adobe style, on a rise above Route 66, with a cow statue on the roof over the entrance and a sign shaped like a covered wagon. 3 Most sources, and the restaurant, give 1967 as the start. The 1997 Campus Times story said 1960. 91

The ties piled up fast. By 1997 “thousands of ties line the ceiling,” the Campus Times reported, and that was already the second batch. Years earlier the room had grown so crowded with ties that they were taken down so a new collection could start. 1

Ownership has changed over the decades. A 2010 San Diego Jewish World story said the San Dimas, Colton and Santee restaurants were all still owned by Louise Tricker. 5 By 2024, the SPEARS Southwest Tour stock-car series was thanking San Dimas co-owner Glenn Davis and Maureen Davis, who have sponsored the series since 2015 and hosted Friday barbecues for race teams at Irwindale Speedway. 6 The pitch hasn’t changed: “a steak dinner at prices a cowboy can afford.” 6

If you go

Pinnacle Peak is at 269 W. Foothill Blvd. in San Dimas, near the Glendora line. Look for the cow on the roof. Come Tuesday through Sunday, book if it’s a weekend dinner, and order the Cowboy with the beans and a cobbler for the table. Wear a tie only if you’re ready to leave it behind.
