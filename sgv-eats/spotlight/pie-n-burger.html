What they serve

The menu the restaurant still points to is the 1963 one: a burger, a special sauce, and pie. Visit Pasadena’s directory adds the breakfast side of the counter. Thin pancakes, pecan rolls, cinnamon rolls, and house-made breads. The patties, that listing says, are made fresh from U.S. prime beef, with a homemade thousand island dressing. Eater LA’s January 2026 report describes the burger with that dressing, iceberg, tomato, and dill pickle, and cheese as a 50-cent add-on. In that same report a burger combination was $18. Prices move. That figure is the one Eater printed.

How long they’ve been there

Their own about page says the restaurant was founded in 1963 at Lake Avenue and California Boulevard, the corner they still list as 913 E. California Blvd. Benny and Florence Foote took it over in 1969. Florence baked the pies. Michael Osborn, the current owner, tells the next chapter on a Pasadena Educational Foundation bio: he started as a night cook in 1972 while at USC, bought part of the business in 1978, and the rest in 1992. The restaurant’s about page puts the same start in the dining room. He first came in as a child with his grandmother, then went to work there in 1972 and stayed.

What they’re known for

The pie case, and the fact that the room still runs on cash. The restaurant says so, and so do Visit Pasadena and Eater. There is an ATM in the place. Eater described a soda machine, a milkshake mixer, an old cash register, and seats at small tables or along the counter facing the kitchen. Visit Pasadena also notes a royal-blue food truck at Pasadena events. Osborn’s foundation bio says the restaurant added catering and that truck, and that the burgers and pies have been on food shows and won awards. This page is not repeating a prize it cannot name.

Their site calls Pasadena the birthplace of the cheeseburger. Eater LA says the proof of that claim has been lost, and that the diner’s place in town has not.
