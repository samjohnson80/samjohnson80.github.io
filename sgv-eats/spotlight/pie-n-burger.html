Pie ’n Burger, Pasadena: bring cash for the burger and the pie

A counter at 913 E. California Blvd. since 1963. What the reviews say to order, what the room still does, and who kept it there. Where the record disagrees, the disagreement stays.

A waitress in Jonathan Gold’s 1993 Los Angeles Times column says, “Hi, howya doin’?” and then, “I put in your order when I saw you coming through the door.” The corner he described is a couple of blocks from Caltech, close enough that “the guy next to you may be reading a long physics proof over his ham and eggs.”7 Adam Bray-Ali, in Pasadena Now in 2014, put it a few blocks from the Seismo Lab and saw a line for seats.15

Why you should go

Order the burger. Eater LA, in January 2026, found homemade Thousand Island, iceberg, tomato, and dill pickle, with cheese at 50 cents, and a patty melt on rye if you want the same beef on grilled bread. A burger combination that month was $18. Kat Thompson wrote, “Is it the best burger in Los Angeles? Probably not,” and then pointed to wholesome ingredients and nostalgia.9 The venue page says the burger comes without cheese unless you ask, at that same 50 cents, and that the room is small and busy at peak hours, so plan on a wait.10

The older write-ups tell you what to ask for. Sue Horton, in LA Weekly in 1999, described toasted buns, lettuce, tomato, onion grilled or raw or both, Thousand Island, and prime meat a local butcher ground every other day.3 Gold wrote the cheese “glossily aloof,” the burger in white paper, and shakes “dense as plutonium,” and called the diner locally famous for big omelets, pancake breakfasts, and crisp, slightly oily hash browns.7 Those are the plates those writers described. Cheese, in the 2026 notes, still costs extra, and you still have to ask.

Then pie, if you have room. Eater’s 2026 report points at banana meringue, or a seasonal slice.9 Elise Thompson, in LAist in 2010, called the fruit pies possibly the best in Los Angeles, blueberry especially, and wrote, “When olallieberry season comes around it is newsworthy.”8 A KCRW Good Food story says Gold called a made-to-order butterscotch cream pie “one of the Lord’s great creations.” The station’s episode page dates that segment September 25, 2015, and the story says the usual topping is meringue, so the butterscotch had to be ordered ahead.1213 Gold’s own 1993 column did not think the pies were great. Later writers did. Osborn’s own choice, told to Pasadena Weekly, is strawberry pie with whipped cream, the slice from a childhood visit with his grandmother.57

Breakfast is a separate window inside the day. The restaurant posts 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. every day except Christmas, with breakfast until 11 a.m. Monday through Friday, noon on Saturday, and 1 p.m. on Sunday. Cash only. There is an ATM in the building.1 Visit Pasadena lists thin pancakes, house-made breads, pecan rolls, and cinnamon rolls, patties it says are made fresh daily from U.S. prime beef, and a homemade thousand island.11 Eater’s 2026 breakfast is eggs, bacon, and buttermilk pancakes.9

Bring cash. Sit at the counter facing the kitchen or at a small table. Eater still found a soda machine, a spindled milkshake mixer, and an old cash register.9 Pasadena Weekly’s 2024 interview keeps the cooking on a flat griddle. The patties are not super-pressed. Osborn makes the Thousand Island. The buns are buttered, with a thick-cut pickle and iceberg. “I pay a premium for the meat,” he said. “It makes a difference.” On the griddle: “I just think it’s the best way to do it.”5 Dan Evans, in a January 13, 2012, Pasadena Sun newsroom roundup, called it “the favorite burger joint of many, if not most, Pasadena natives,” and ordered a cheeseburger, fries, and boysenberry pie. He thought the fries were better elsewhere.147

Paul Little, president and CEO of the Pasadena Chamber of Commerce, told the Weekly that Pie ’n Burger “is always on the top ten burgers in the country lists.” That is his claim. The home page says Pasadena “is heralded as the birthplace of the cheeseburger,” and that the diner honors the tradition. It does not say this kitchen invented the sandwich. Eater’s venue page says the truth of that birthplace claim “has been lost to history.”5110

Who kept the counter

The about page dates the founding to 1963, at Lake Avenue and California Boulevard, and calls the idea “honest food made with care.” Benny and Florence Foote took over in 1969, he on the grill, she baking the pies. The page does not name a 1963 founder.2 Horton told a different start. An unnamed first owner bought the 1930s recipes of a Long Beach couple whose diner he admired, handed the place to those recipe originators, and they passed it to Osborn. She does not say the couple were the Footes.3 The home page says many recipes date to 1963, not the 1930s.1 Wikipedia says the Footes founded it in 1963 and that Osborn bought the rest from them in 1992 so they could retire. That contradicts the about page’s 1969 takeover, and it contradicts Horton.16

Osborn is the owner the restaurant names now. The about page has him walking in at 9 with his grandmother, then taking a 1972 job at USC he thought was temporary: “dentistry’s loss became Pasadena’s gain.”2 Horton set that first meal just after the 1963 opening. The family lived in Arizona. His grandmother lived in Arcadia and worked at the old Bullocks Pasadena around the corner.3 A foundation bio in his voice says he was born in Scottsdale in 1954, moved to California in 1966, and finished Blair High in 1972. He was a night cook, pre-dental, decided against dentistry after one year of college, graduated in 1976, became manager, bought part of the business in 1978, and bought the rest in 1992. He married in 1985 and has three grown children.4

“My dad told me they were looking for a cook three days a week. I got the job and I never left.”

That is Osborn, to Pasadena Weekly in September 2024. The Weekly also said he was 70 and that the first visit was in 1963, at 9, which fits a 1954 birth. It wrote that he bought Pie ’n Burger in 1978, and did not say the purchase was only a part.5 Pasadena Now, on August 13, 2026, spelled him Osborne and wrote that he told the Design Commission the first visit was 1964. Nine in 1963 is ten in 1964. Pasadena Now and Wikipedia spell the name Osborne. The restaurant’s site spells Osborn.616

What he told Horton he would not change was the shopping list. “I buy Kraft mayonnaise because that’s what we’ve always used,” he said. “And Gold Medal flour and C&H sugar.” He had added a vegetarian burger and, at breakfast, fresh fruit and cereal. The Formica counter, the swivel stools, and the longtime waitresses were still there.3 The about page says the Foote recipes remain, along with employees whose families have worked there for generations.2 The bio says the one restaurant later added catering and a food truck. Visit Pasadena describes that truck as royal blue.411

The years that do not match

The Weekly called September 19, 2024, the shop’s 53rd anniversary, and his 53rd year behind the counter. He would mark it with “a couple good bottles of wine” and “a cheeseburger and strawberry pie with whipped cream.” “This place is like home to me.” “I’ve been here forever.” “I grew up here and have never lived more than five miles from here.”5 A 53rd year of work from 1972 fits the bio. It does not fit a 1963 opening. The about page and the bio both describe the restaurant as more than 60 years old. Pasadena Now’s “55 years,” in 2026, counts the job, not the founding.426

That August hearing was a preliminary consultation. The commission took no vote. Osborn, under the paper’s spelling, opposed a five-story senior building proposed at 540 S. Lake Ave. “We’re sort of an iconic place,” he said. People who have not eaten there, he said, come for the “classic diner look.” The project “just dwarfs it, and … makes it really irrelevant.” Nothing in that story says the diner is closing. The posted day is still 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., except Christmas.61

If you go

Bring cash to 913 E. California Blvd., Pasadena. The counter faces the kitchen. Ask for the cheese if you want it. Leave room for a slice. The map is here.19
