Ducks, San Gabriel: $18.95 katsu curry and soba sets from a family kitchen that has never once served duck

Shojiro and Midori Saeki opened Ducks in a Las Tunas Drive strip mall in 1995. Their daughter Natsumi runs it now, on her dad’s recipes. Go for the pork katsu curry, a soba set, and a room lined with customers’ rubber ducks.

“Duck has never appeared on the menu at Ducks.” That is the first line of Eater LA’s 2023 profile of this San Gabriel restaurant, and it is still true. 1 The name comes from a family habit. “We lived really close to Almansor Park and we used to go all the time to feed the ducks,” owner Natsumi Saeki told Eater. “When my dad opened [the restaurant], it was like our family was feeding the ducks.” 1

Thirty-one years later, the family is still at it. Ducks is a 10-table room in a quiet strip mall on Las Tunas Drive, cooking the Japanese food that families eat at home: katsu curry, soba and udon, donburi, tempura. 1 When Eater LA went back in May 2026, it called Ducks “a multigenerational family operation with cozy bowls of soba noodles and homestyle katsu curry.” 2

Why you should go

Order the pork katsu curry. It’s $18.95 on the current menu. 3 Eater’s Kat Thompson calls it “my most reliable entree for years now.” She writes that the cutlet “gets pounded thin without drying out” under “a satisfyingly crunchy panko crust.” 2 If you like heat, take her tip: order the curry extra spicy, or ask for a few drops of the house chile oil. 2

Or make it a soba set. The soba comes in hot or chilled broth, which Thompson says makes it right for winter or summer, and the mixed tempura on the side adds the crunch. 2 Hot or cold soba with tempura is $25.40. At lunch, a cold soba with a mini curry is $17.40. 3 To start, get the fried chikuwa fish cakes ($8.95). Thompson says their batter has “craggy, sharp strands” that “look like pulled sugar.” 23

When you want to splurge. The unagi bowl is broiled eel glazed in a sweet and savory tare, and it comes with miso soup for $31.95. 3 Thompson says the eel “arrives so tender it yields to the lightest touch.” 2

Try the oddballs. Shojiro Saeki’s original menu included Japanese spaghetti in a tomato or curry sauce, and it is still there. Curry spaghetti, topped with half a boiled egg, is $15.50. 13 MySGV calls the katsudon the dish Ducks is famous for. It’s pork katsu cooked with egg and sweet soy over rice, and it costs $18.95. 43 Every meal comes with a simple cabbage salad, and Eater called it “something to look forward to.” 1

What to expect. Thompson says the dining room “feels like eating in an auntie’s living room.” Rubber ducks given by longtime customers line every windowsill and shelf. Portraits of Natsumi’s cat, Chibi, hang on the walls, and there are shelves of manga to read. 2 The room is small. Eater once described a deep waitlist on a busy dinner night. 1 On weekdays, lunch ends at 1:45 p.m. and dinner starts at 5. Weekends run straight through. 5 There’s Japanese beer and small bottles of sake. 3 On Yelp it has more than 900 reviews. 5

The backstory: feeding the ducks

Shojiro and Midori Saeki took over an Indian restaurant called Silver Spoons and opened Ducks in May 1995. Natsumi was 7. 14 At the time, Eater notes, most people thought of Japanese food as sushi. Shojiro wanted to serve what Japanese families ate at home. They never advertised. “We didn’t do any advertisements; it was strictly word of mouth,” Natsumi says. “The menu items are all my dad’s recipes, but my mom made them extra-special.” 1

The whole family worked there. “My aunt worked here, my cousin worked here — it was a whole family ordeal,” she told Eater. “Customers would come in and help [me and my sister] with our homework.” 1

Natsumi went to San Gabriel High School and Cal State Long Beach. 4 She had a career in health care administration when her parents talked about retiring in 2015 and raised the idea of selling. She didn’t weigh it. “I never thought about it. I just quit my other job.” 1 Her father was a stickler about his recipes and found it hard to let go. Then her mother had a stroke in 2017, and Natsumi took charge. Her biggest fear was losing the regulars once they no longer saw her parents in the kitchen. “But that wasn’t the case,” she said. 1

During the pandemic, a popular Facebook group called SGV Eats (no relation to this site) urged its members to order takeout from Ducks. Customers ate karaage and Japanese spaghetti in their cars in the parking lot. “It was really cool because not only did [SGV Eats] save us through COVID, but it also boosted us for the following year because it introduced many new customers,” Saeki told Eater. 1

Her parents still come in as customers, and they don’t get special treatment. One busy night with a long waitlist, they took their order to go, and her mother “sneakily heisted a few beers from the fridge on the way out.” 1 In 2026 Eater wrote that the offerings “haven’t changed too much in 30 years.” 2

If you go

Ducks is at 1381 E. Las Tunas Dr. in San Gabriel. Go any day from Wednesday to Sunday. Order the pork katsu curry, extra spicy if you like heat, or a soba set with tempura. Look for the rubber ducks in the windows. Thirty-one years in, the family is still feeding the ducks.
