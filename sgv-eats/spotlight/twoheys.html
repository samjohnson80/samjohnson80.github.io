Twohey’s Restaurant: the diner that named its burger after someone who said “Oh Stink-O”

Since 1943: pickles, onions, buttermilk onion rings, hand-dipped sundaes — and a South Pasadena comeback after the Alhambra lease ended.

Jack Twohey overheard it first. A woman at his 37-seat diner caught a whiff of a hamburger piled with pickles and onions and blurted something that stuck: “Oh stinko!” He trademarked the Little Stinko — a clothes-pin-and-tear mascot — in 1943, the same year he and Jean Twohey opened the place on Arroyo Parkway in Pasadena.12 Eighty-plus years later, the Original Stinko is still on the menu at 424 Fair Oaks Avenue in South Pasadena, same pickles, same onions, same punchline.

That naming origin is the hook. The reason to go today is simpler: a family diner that kept roughly 80% of its original menu through a lease loss, a gut remodel, and a COVID delay — and still makes buttermilk-battered onion rings and house bittersweet fountain toppings by hand.52

Why you should go

Order the Original Stinko. Pickles, onions, old-fashioned bun — the namesake burger. Third-party menu aggregators list it around $16; the restaurant’s official dinner page describes the dish but does not publish prices online, so treat that figure as a guide and confirm on the board.79 Pasadena Now’s Weekendr calls the Stinko the classic.8 Lifelong regular John Kovak, who told the South Pasadenan he had been coming since age 12, still named “the Stinko burger, with pickles and onions” as his favorite.5

Add the buttermilk onion rings. They have been a house signature since the early days. Aggregators list a plate around $12; the official dinner menu notes a ring-combo add-on for $4.95.79 If you want one more burger, the Everything (Tillamook cheddar and “1001 Island”) is the other press favorite.89

Finish at the soda fountain. Twohey’s Signature Sundae uses house bittersweet, milk chocolate, and caramel toppings the retail shoppe also sells — small and large sizes list around $13–16 on aggregators.72 This is hand-dipped fountain work, not a button-push shake machine.

Go Saturday through Monday or Wednesday through Sunday — never Tuesday. The official hours grid and the South Pasadenan’s 80th-anniversary write-up agree: closed Tuesdays; Monday/Wednesday/Thursday 9–9; Friday 9–10; Saturday 8–10; Sunday 8–9.62 Breakfast runs until 3 PM on open days.2 Expect a vintage-inspired lunch counter and soda fountain, a full bar, outdoor patios, and roughly 199 seats (patio about 90, private room about 25).21

What makes it different. It is not a chain. One family business since 1943, and only a second ownership group — Tanya Christos, Jim Christos, and Greg Mallis — since 1997.51 After the Alhambra lease ended, they spent nearly five years finding and gutting a new home so the Stinko could stay in the San Gabriel Valley.54

From Arroyo to Fair Oaks

Jack and Jean opened in 1943 with 37 seats on Arroyo Parkway (some sources say Arroyo Boulevard — same corridor) in Pasadena.12 In the 1950s the diner relocated to Huntington Drive and Atlantic Boulevard in Alhambra and became a classic carhop — drive-up service that lasted into the mid-1970s; a 1977 renovation ended the carhop era.123 Sources disagree on the exact Alhambra move year: the official Our Story page says 1955; Wikipedia says 1951; a 2017 Los Angeles Times piece phrased it as “10 years later” after 1943 (about 1953); the South Pasadenan simply says “in the 1950s.” Prefer the decade unless you are quoting the restaurant’s own date.1342

In 1997 the Twohey family sold to the Christos/Mallis group — described as the only second-hand owners.54 In late 2017 the Alhambra lease could not be extended; the location closed around February 1, 2018.4 The owners took the former Carmine’s / Ace Hardware complex space at 424 Fair Oaks, tore the interior down to the four walls, and waited through COVID delays. Soft open: October 10, 2020. Chamber ribbon cutting: mid-November 2020.523

“We’re very grateful and blessed… It did take us a long time to get here, but they welcomed us with open arms.”

That is co-owner Tanya Christos, speaking at the South Pasadena ribbon cutting.5 Outgoing Mayor Bob Joe added the civic version: “Twoheys is a tradition… there are people remembering their first day eating there, their anniversaries, birthdays, and proposals.”5 In September 2023 the restaurant marked 80 years with a five-day celebration and recognitions from congressional, state, county, and city offices.23

If you go

Twohey’s Restaurant is at 424 Fair Oaks Avenue in South Pasadena (map). Order the Original Stinko with onion rings, save room for a Signature Sundae, and skip Tuesday. Follow along at @twoheys_restaurant. The clothes-pin mascot is still on the wall. The punchline is still on the bun.
