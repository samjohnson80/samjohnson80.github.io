# How to write an SGV Eats spotlight

The reader should finish wanting to go. Selling stays inside the sourcing rules: every claim has a footnote, quotes are verbatim and attributed, disagreements are stated, and nothing says Sam ate there. No fake urgency, no invented countdown, no prize you cannot name.

Daily spotlights run about 600–1,000 words. The Pie ’n Burger feature is the long example, about 900–1,400. Count the prose, headings included. Leave out the sources list, the facts block, the nav, and the footer. Follow this checklist either way.

## Checklist

- **Headline.** David Ogilvy, *Confessions of an Advertising Man* and *Ogilvy on Advertising*: the headline does most of the work, so put the restaurant and a specific reason to go in it, including the city. Facts sell. Long copy is fine when it stays interesting.
- **Opening.** Joseph Sugarman, *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*: the first sentence exists to get the next one read (the slippery slide). Start with a concrete scene from a source. Use sensory detail only when a published account already described it.
- **Desire.** Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*: copy channels a want people already have. Name that want (a straight burger, breakfast, a counter) and show how this place meets it. Do not invent a craving.
- **Specifics.** Claude Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising*: a price, an hour, a topping, or a named review beats “best” or “amazing.” If a superlative appears, it is someone’s quoted words, attributed, not the page’s own claim.
- **Memory.** Chip and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick*: keep one simple idea, one unexpected sourced detail, concrete objects, a checkable source, a feeling that comes from the story, and the story itself.
- **Proof.** Robert Cialdini, *Influence*: social proof and authority mean cited press and years in business. Scarcity means a real limit a source states (cash only, a small room, a posted hour, a wait at peak). Do not invent a deadline.
- **The reader.** Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand*: the reader is the hero and the restaurant is the guide. Give a plan, not a lecture.

## Required sections

1. **Why you should go.** What to order, from their menu and from what reviewers single out. When to go, from posted hours. What to expect (cash, counter, the room). What is different, in sourced specifics. Say so when reviewers disagree.
2. **The backstory.** Founding, ownership to today, what stayed, press, and the place in town if sources support it.
3. **If you go.** A closing nudge with the street address and a map link. Invite the visit. Do not say the place is about to disappear unless a source says that, and do not turn a hearing or a rumor into a last chance.

## What not to do

- Do not write a taste, a smell, or a scene that is not in a cited source.
- Do not smooth over two dates or two owners by picking one.
- Do not display any phone number except 626-799-7813. Copy the `tel:` and `sms:` hrefs from the template unchanged.
- One soft line about Sam on the page: “Own a restaurant in the SGV and want to be featured? Text Sam.”
