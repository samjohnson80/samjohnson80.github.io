The extra million does not follow the page underneath.

A lease and a certificate can both show two million, or five, sitting above the restaurant. The umbrella is its own form. If the pour, the fight, or the car is excluded or capped underneath, the edition I can read does not climb past that.

By Sam Johnson, account executive at Shaw, Moses, Mendenhall & Associates. Written for owners, not for insurance people. I am not your lawyer.

The PDF usually arrives from the landlord, and the sentence is short. Umbrella, or excess. Two million, or five. Additional insured. Following form. You already have a general liability policy that says one million. Someone prints the certificate. The Friday deadline feels handled.

I have sat with that certificate in Monrovia, Pasadena, Arcadia, and Alhambra. A dining room that pours. A bar that becomes a bar after ten. A café that puts a Friday catering run in somebody’s own car. The extra million is a different contract from the policy underneath it. On the form I can open, it stops where that contract says it stops. The certificate will still show the big number.

The rest of the market, property and the comp benchmark included, is the fall note. This one is the layer above the dining room.

The line that did not get cheaper

The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers asked brokers about the second quarter of 2026. Umbrella premiums rose by an average of 5.3%. That was the highest increase of any line in the survey, and the thirty-fifth quarter in a row of increases for umbrella. The Council said the quarter reversed a moderating trend that had started in the second quarter of 2025. Commercial auto was next, up 4.5%. The write-up says the pairing was not a coincidence. Nuclear verdicts tied to commercial auto accidents were one of the main sources of difficulty for the umbrella line, and that difficulty showed up as lower limits and reduced capacity. Forty percent of the brokers who answered said umbrella capacity had decreased.

Read the survey as national. It is every kind of account those brokers happen to place. It is not a count of cafés on Colorado Boulevard. Carriers, in the same write-up, were easing property and workers’ compensation partly to offset umbrella and auto. That is why a softer headline about “the market” can arrive in the same month as a firmer umbrella renewal. Your building and your layer above it are allowed to move in different directions.

A wholesale index says the increases have slowed, and that they have not stopped. CRC Group’s Excess + Umbrella REDY Index for the second quarter of 2026 puts first-half 2026 excess and umbrella renewals at an average of 6.3% to 10.7%, down from the 12% to 16% range of a year earlier. CRC says the rate of increase is decreasing, that brokers are seeing more quotes, and that clean accounts are getting more of the relief. The same page says social inflation, larger jury verdicts, and third-party litigation funding are still pressuring severity in the excess layers, and that underwriters are still selective on the tough accounts. Carriers willing to deploy $25 million or more in a single layer remain scarce, it says, and most first-layer towers cap at $5 million to $10 million. The index is brokerage accounts that renewed in the same month as the year before, with the same total limits. CRC removed the top and bottom 1% and says a single account usually differs from the average. I will not turn either survey into a price for a place in Duarte.

“Social inflation” is the name the industry uses for the slice of this that is not the price of eggs or glass. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners, on a page updated December 19, 2025, describes it as insurers’ liability claims costs increasing above general economic inflation. The page says that increase is generally thought to come from litigation in which plaintiffs seek large monetary relief, and that a nuclear verdict is generally a jury verdict above $10 million. I am using their definition. I am not using the phrase as a mood.

The 2025 count is in Marathon Strategies’ 2026 report. Nearly 200 lawsuits against a corporate defendant produced a verdict of at least $10 million. Marathon says that was a 40.7% increase over 2024, the largest number it has identified in a single year since 2009, and that the verdicts summed to over $25.6 billion. Verdicts the firm calls thermonuclear, its word for more than $100 million, topped 40 for the second year in a row. The state table lists California at $2,583,818,372 across 25 verdicts, and it names pharmaceuticals as California’s top sector. Hotels, restaurants, or leisure facilities are one national bucket in the report, at $2.3 billion. That bucket is not Pasadena. The verdict table puts an $831 million dram-shop verdict against Koozies Ice House, in Texas, in hotels, restaurants, and leisure. The report’s Florida section describes a $779 million wrongful-death verdict against an internet café. A dram shop is a liquor lawsuit. The license, and when a California restaurant can still be sued over a pour, is the Type 41 and Type 47 note. I am not retrying it here.

The local verdict I will use is smaller, and it is still a chain, not a café in Sierra Madre. The Los Angeles Times reported on March 15, 2025, that a Los Angeles County jury had awarded $50 million to Michael Garcia, a Postmates delivery driver. On February 8, 2020, at a Starbucks drive-through in Exposition Park, hot teas in a carrier spilled and burned him. The Times said the burns were severe and that he underwent skin grafts. Starbucks told the Times it planned to appeal, that it disagreed with the jury on fault, and that it believed the damages were excessive. A notice of settlement of the entire case, Judicial Council form CM-200, was filed June 20, 2025, in Los Angeles Superior Court case 20STCV10214, Garcia v. Starbucks Corporation. The copy I read marks the settlement unconditional. It does not state an amount. A Los Angeles jury was willing to say $50 million about a drink handed through a window to a delivery driver. That is the severity people in this layer are pricing. The crash, when the car is your employee’s, is a different page. That one is the Thanksgiving note.

The lease can say following form. The schedule is what the form actually follows.

The umbrella is its own policy

The form I can read in public is CU 00 01 04 13, the commercial liability umbrella coverage form, as posted by Nationwide Excess and Surplus. A later edition can move a sentence. A carrier can file its own words. The form number on your declarations is the one that counts.

On that edition, the company pays the insured’s ultimate net loss in excess of the retained limit, for bodily injury or property damage the insurance applies to. The retained limit is the available limits of the underlying insurance scheduled in the declarations, or the self-insured retention, whichever applies. Underlying insurance means the policies listed in that schedule. A policy that is not on the schedule is not underlying.

The self-insured retention is the dollar amount listed in the declarations that the insured pays before this insurance applies, and only for an occurrence or offense the underlying insurance does not cover. It does not apply to an occurrence that underlying insurance would have covered but for the exhaustion of the underlying limits. If someone tells you the umbrella “drops down” for everything, read those two sentences apart. Something the underlying never covered is the retention, and you pay it. Something the underlying did cover, once those limits are used up paying claims, is not the retention.

Then the sentence I want on the table before anyone argues about the big number. If any other limit, such as a sublimit, is specified in the underlying insurance, this insurance does not apply to bodily injury or property damage arising out of that exposure unless that limit is specified in the declarations under the schedule of underlying insurance. A smaller cap on the fight, on liquor, or on any other exposure the underlying policy limits separately, stays off the umbrella until that smaller limit is scheduled. The million on the general liability declarations is not that schedule. The certificate is not that schedule.

Defense, on the same opening. The company has the right and duty to defend when the underlying insurance does not provide coverage, or when the underlying limits have been exhausted. The duty ends when the company has used up the umbrella’s applicable limit paying judgments or settlements. When the underlying limits have been used up in the payment of judgments or settlements, the duty to defend transfers to the umbrella company. A manuscript form can say something else. I am reading 04 13.

Anyone who is an additional insured under a policy of underlying insurance is automatically an insured under this umbrella. If a contract requires that coverage, the most the umbrella will pay on behalf of the additional insured is the amount the contract requires, less what the underlying pays, or the umbrella limit, whichever is less. Additional insured coverage on the umbrella will not be broader than the underlying. The landlord you added on the general liability endorsement is not a larger landlord up here than that endorsement made them.

You have to keep the underlying insurance in full effect, without reducing coverage or limits, except that the aggregate may be reduced by payment of claims, settlements, or judgments to which this insurance applies. That exhaustion is not a failure to maintain. If you do let the underlying lapse, or you cut the limit, the umbrella is not invalidated. It applies as if the underlying insurance were in full effect. The hole between what you used to schedule and what you kept is yours. You have to notify the umbrella company in writing, as soon as practicable, if any underlying policy is cancelled, not renewed, replaced, or terminated, or if the limits or the scope change.

Bankruptcy of the underlying insurer gets the same shape. The umbrella will not replace that policy. It applies as if the underlying insurance were in full effect. You do not receive a free primary policy because the company underneath failed.

Other insurance, on this edition: the umbrella is excess over, and shall not contribute with, any other insurance, except insurance written specifically as excess over the umbrella. A lease that asks for a primary and noncontributory umbrella is asking for a sentence this condition does not contain. Ask to see the endorsement that changes it. The lease’s request is not the endorsement.

The pour, and the fight, have to be scheduled

Liquor on CU 00 01 04 13 is an exclusion when you are in the business of manufacturing, distributing, selling, serving, or furnishing alcoholic beverages. The exclusion is bodily injury or property damage for causing or contributing to intoxication, for furnishing alcohol to a person under the legal drinking age or under the influence, or because of a statute, ordinance, or regulation on the sale, gift, distribution, or use of alcoholic beverages. It still applies if the lawsuit is pleaded as supervision, hiring, employment, training, or monitoring, or as providing or failing to provide a ride to someone who may be under the influence, when the occurrence was one of those three. Letting a guest bring their own bottle, by itself, is not that business. A bar is. A restaurant that pours is. The license side is the liquor note.

The exception is why this note exists. The exclusion does not apply to the extent valid underlying insurance for those liquor risks exists, or would have existed but for the exhaustion of the underlying bodily-injury and property-damage limits. To that extent, the umbrella follows the same provisions, exclusions, and limitations in the applicable underlying insurance, unless the umbrella directs otherwise.

Those are two different gaps.

If the liquor policy is not on the schedule, the exclusion stands. The extra million does not become liquor liability because general liability is scheduled. General liability already sent the pour away. That is the first page of the liquor note.

If the liquor policy is on the schedule, the umbrella follows that policy, including what the liquor policy takes out or caps. An assault exclusion on the liquor form is an exclusion the umbrella follows for the liquor risk. A sublimit on the liquor form is the earlier sentence: the umbrella does not apply to that exposure unless the sublimit itself is scheduled. The fight, the 2026 ISO endorsements, and the defense sentence on the underlying policy are the New Year’s Eve note. The layer above can be loyal to the smaller page. I have not opened a 2026 assault endorsement written for the umbrella. If a quote adds one, I want the form number, and I will read it before I describe it.

The car, the cook, and the person you let go

Auto on the same umbrella does not apply to bodily injury or property damage from the ownership, maintenance, or use of an auto that is not a covered auto. A covered auto means only those autos to which the underlying insurance applies. Hired and non-owned auto, when it is actually on an underlying policy and that policy is on the schedule, is how a car the restaurant does not own becomes a covered auto under that definition. If the endorsement is missing underneath, the umbrella does not invent it. The same auto exclusion takes out first-party physical damage, no-fault, personal injury protection or auto medical payments, and uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. The other person’s injuries are the question. The employee’s bumper is not. Who is an insured on the hired and non-owned endorsement, and why a delivery app is a different driver, is the delivery note. This note is only whether the layer above can see that endorsement.

Employer’s liability is the employee lawsuit that is not the comp benefit. The umbrella excludes bodily injury to an employee arising out of and in the course of employment, or out of performing duties related to the business, and the exclusion applies whether you are liable as an employer or in any other capacity. It then lifts, to the extent valid underlying insurance for those employer’s liability risks exists or would have existed but for exhaustion of the underlying bodily-injury limits. To that extent the umbrella follows the provisions, exclusions, and limitations of that underlying insurance, unless the umbrella says otherwise. Those limits are printed on the workers’ compensation policy. If they are not on the umbrella’s schedule, the follow does not start.

Workers’ compensation itself is a separate exclusion, and on this edition that exclusion has no follow-form sentence. Any obligation of the insured under a workers’ compensation, disability benefits, or unemployment compensation law, or any similar law, is out. The umbrella does not become the comp policy when the comp benefits are exhausted. How the California benchmark moved on September 1 is the comp note.

Employment-related practices are out on their own exclusion: refusal to hire, termination, and the practices the form lists, including coercion, demotion, discipline, defamation, harassment, humiliation, discrimination, and malicious prosecution. The exclusion applies before, during, and after the employment, and whether you are liable as an employer or in any other capacity. The paragraph I read does not lift when some other policy exists. A firing, harassment, or discrimination claim is the employment-practices note. A taller general liability limit does not move it.

One limits sentence, because owners ask whether one bad night uses the whole umbrella. On this edition the aggregate is the most the company will pay for ultimate net loss under Coverage A, except ultimate net loss for bodily injury or property damage arising out of the ownership, maintenance, or use of a covered auto, together with Coverage B, which is personal and advertising injury. A covered auto sits outside that aggregate. It still sits inside the each-occurrence limit. Read the limits section on your form before you assume one crash and one slip share one bucket.

Excess follows, and then it refuses to be broader

Leases say “umbrella or excess” as if the words were twins. On the ISO forms I can open, they are not.

CX 00 01 04 13, the commercial excess liability coverage form, opens by following. The insurance follows the same provisions, exclusions, and limitations contained in the applicable controlling underlying insurance, unless the excess form directs otherwise. Where those provisions differ or conflict, the excess form applies. The coverage will not be broader than the controlling underlying insurance. The sublimit sentence is there too. If any other limit, such as a sublimit, is specified in the controlling underlying insurance, this insurance does not apply to injury or damage arising out of that exposure unless that limit is specified in the declarations under the schedule of controlling underlying insurance.

An excess policy can be loyal to a thin page underneath, because loyalty is the design. An exclusion on the liquor form, or a cap on the fight, is an exclusion or a cap above it, unless the excess form says it supersedes that page. The copy I opened is clean at the start and messy in the middle, so I am using the opening I could read. Bring yours. I will read the pages, not the title.

The umbrella has its own exclusions, and it follows underlying on the risks where an exclusion is lifted. Liquor and employer’s liability are two of those. It can also defend a suit the underlying does not cover, subject to the retention. The excess form I read ties the duty to defend to exhaustion of the controlling underlying limits. I will not call either shape the drop-down your lease asked for until both pages are on the table.

The lease is where the number comes from

I cannot point you to a California statute that tells a San Gabriel Valley restaurant to buy an umbrella. The Labor Code requirement I send people to is workers’ compensation, and that is the other note. When an umbrella number exists, it is in the lease. I said to read that section first in the note on opening. Here is what the section looks like when a California landlord typed it. Neither lease is a dining room. I am showing the shape. I am not borrowing either dollar figure for Huntington Drive.

AeroVironment’s lease with BCORE Defender CA1W03, LLC, dated March 11, 2022, for 85 Moreland Road in Simi Valley, requires an umbrella or excess policy of not less than $5,000,000. The policy “shall be in ‘following form’ and shall provide that if the underlying aggregate is exhausted, the excess coverage will drop down as primary insurance.” It shall include coverage for additional insureds. The landlord may reasonably increase the amounts, on written notice, if the tenant’s operations change or the landlord reasonably decides the amounts are inadequate. The limits the tenant carries do not limit the tenant’s liability under the lease. On the same page, automobile liability includes owned, hired, or non-owned automobiles, at a combined single limit of not less than $1,000,000, and employer’s liability is at least $1,000,000. A deductible or a self-insured retention above $25,000 needs the landlord’s consent.

Zuora’s lease with 101 Redwood Shores LLC, dated March 19, 2019, for 101 Redwood Shores Parkway in Redwood City, requires umbrella or excess liability over the general liability, the automobile liability, and the employer’s liability, at not less than $15,000,000 each occurrence and $15,000,000 aggregate. If the tenant owns no automobiles, non-owned and hired auto liability of not less than $1,000,000 is still required. Employer’s liability is not less than $1,000,000 per accident. The minimums do not limit the tenant’s liability for its acts or omissions. The landlord may modify the requirements for changes in occupancy, exposure, or the insurance marketplace, in line with what owners of comparable buildings are then requiring. Host liquor is required only in the event the tenant or its employees bring alcohol onto the premises, the lot, or the common areas. The $15 million, in the sentence I read, sits over general liability, auto, and employer’s liability. It does not sit over that host-liquor line.

That is the reason a landlord writes a specific number. The landlord expects to be named when someone is hurt in the space. The primary limit is a floor the landlord wants to see on a certificate. It is not a cap on what you owe. The next layer is supposed to follow the policies underneath and to answer when the aggregate underneath is used up. The number is specific because the landlord picked one, and because both of these leases keep a way to pick a different one later. Five million in Simi Valley and fifteen million in Redwood City are both real exhibits. A one-room café in Duarte may be asked for one million, or for nothing above the primary. Read your exhibit.

The three English words “following form” do not do the schedule’s job. The Simi Valley lease asks for a following-form policy that drops down as primary insurance when the underlying aggregate is exhausted. CU 00 01 follows underlying where the liquor and employer’s liability exclusions are lifted, and then it follows that underlying policy’s exclusions and limitations. CX 00 01 follows, and it will not be broader than the underlying. A sublimit that is not scheduled is not followed, on either form I read. A certificate that types $5 million does not schedule an assault cap. If the lease also says the umbrella is primary and noncontributory, that is a further sentence. The other-insurance condition on CU 00 01 04 13 says the umbrella is excess and noncontributory until an endorsement changes it.

What I want on the table

This is the order, while the renewal, or the landlord’s Friday, is still a date.

  1. The insurance exhibit. The dollar limit. Whether it says umbrella, excess, or either. Whether it says following form, drop down, primary and noncontributory, and additional insured. Which policies it says the layer sits over. Whether the landlord can raise the number. The exhibit’s limits do not cap what you owe under the lease. The opening note is the longer version of reading the lease before you buy the policy.
  2. The declarations, the schedule of underlying, and the form number. General liability. Liquor, if you pour, at the limit the liquor policy actually carries. Auto, including hired and non-owned if anyone drives for the restaurant, and that includes you. Employer’s liability, matched to the limits on the workers’ compensation policy. On CU 00 01, a policy that is not listed in the schedule is not underlying insurance.
  3. Every sublimit. Assault, battery, liquor, or any other exposure the underlying policy caps below the main limit. Is that smaller number scheduled on the umbrella or the excess. If it is not, both CU 00 01 04 13 and CX 00 01 04 13 say the layer does not apply to that exposure.
  4. The fight, on three forms. General liability, liquor, and the layer above. The New Year’s Eve note is the first two. If the quote adds an assault endorsement on the umbrella or the excess, I want the form number in writing. I will read that page before I tell you the extra million follows the room.
  5. The maintenance sentence. If the underlying limit is cut, or the policy lapses, the umbrella I read applies as if the underlying were still in full effect. You keep the gap. The condition also says to tell the umbrella company, in writing and as soon as practicable, when the underlying changes.
  6. The landlord, on the umbrella. On CU 00 01, an additional insured on the underlying is an insured on the umbrella, the coverage is not broader than the underlying, and the pay is capped at what the contract requires, less the underlying, or at the umbrella limit, whichever is less. The certificate is the piece of paper. The endorsement is the coverage.

Asking these questions can move the premium. The first section is why I will not promise that it stays put. If the schedule already matches the lease and the way the place actually runs, I will say that too. Coverage depends on the wording of the policy that is actually issued.

Lunch is the easier table. Bring the exhibit and the schedule. Tell me whether you pour, whether anyone drives, and what the landlord actually wrote. I’ll buy.
