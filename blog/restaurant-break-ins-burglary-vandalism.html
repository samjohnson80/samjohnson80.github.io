The brick is not the till.

A smashed door, a speaker that walked out, and the cash in the drawer are three different lines. On the businessowners form I opened, the door can be the building. The till is not covered property until a money limit is typed. The nights you stay closed have a waiting period. Here is the page to read before the holiday parties, and the morning after if the glass is already on the sidewalk.

By Sam Johnson, account executive at Shaw, Moses, Mendenhall & Associates. Written for owners, not for insurance people. I am not your lawyer.

The second half of November is when the door is doing two jobs. It is how people see the room, and it is glass. The drawer is heavier than it was in October, because the parties pay in a mix of cards and cash and the closer is the one who knows the alarm code. I would rather sort the form while the glass is still in the frame. Monrovia, Arcadia, Pasadena, Covina, the rest of the valley. The holiday does not sort a broken door, a stolen speaker, and a till into three files. You do, the same morning, or the glass company has already swept the only evidence you had.

Two nights, and a Saturday in Pasadena

These are news reports. They are not claims I handled, and I am not turning a reporter’s paragraph into your limit.

On October 25, a Sunday, Will Conybeare’s story, which I read on Yahoo the next day, describes a break-in at La Casita Del Sol on the 4100 block of East Live Oak Avenue in Arcadia. The story says it was about 4:45 a.m. The restaurant told him a hooded person broke the glass front door, stepped through, went through drawers, and left with a speaker and a cell phone the employees had been using in place of a landline. The restaurant said three other businesses on that street were hit, and that this was the second break-in at the restaurant. The story says Arcadia authorities had not commented when it was published. I am leaving it there. A police confirmation is a different sentence from an owner’s account, and the story I opened has the second one.

CBS Los Angeles, updated April 28, 2026, reported a different night. Covina police, CBS wrote, were looking for a man they believed had committed four commercial burglaries on April 22: Mauricio’s Mexican Food, Sam’s Peruvian Restaurant, The Spot Teriyaki and Burger, and El Patito Panaderia. CBS wrote that he used a brick on the front glass doors and stole cash from the registers and tills. A brick, a door, and a drawer. That is the shape of the question I get at lunch.

The safe is the third shape. ABC7, on March 30, 2026, reported that four people got out of a car early the previous Saturday morning, smashed a window at Highlight Coffee in Pasadena, and left with a small safe. ABC7 wrote that they were in and out in less than two minutes. A window is one piece of property. A safe is another. Whatever currency was inside the safe is a third, and on the form I opened it is the one the building limit does not answer.

The brick is a cause of loss. The till is not the building limit.

The door, the glass, and the speaker

The package I can open in public is BP 00 03 07 13, the businessowners coverage form. Covered causes of loss are direct physical loss, unless the loss is excluded or limited. I did not read an exclusion that takes vandalism away, and I did not read an exclusion that takes a stranger’s theft away. What I did read, and I will come back to it, is a dishonesty exclusion for you and for your people. A person with a brick is the open peril. An employee who pockets the close is the other note. A carrier can file its own form. A later edition can move a word. The forms list on the policy that was issued is the one that counts.

A door is a building question first. Buildings, on this edition, mean the buildings and structures at the described premises, including completed additions, fixtures, and permanently installed machinery and equipment. A glass door in the wall is that building, if a building limit is on the declarations and the door is part of what you insure. If you are the tenant and no building limit is shown, business personal property still includes exterior building glass, and the glass has to be owned by you or in your care, custody, or control. The same business-personal-property section includes tenant’s improvements and betterments: fixtures, alterations, installations, or additions made a part of the building you occupy but do not own, that you acquired or made at your expense and cannot legally remove. A storefront you paid for, and cannot take with you, is the shape of that sentence. A storefront the landlord insured on the landlord’s policy is the landlord’s building. I will not decide your lease from Huntington Drive. The lease is where the note on a new restaurant starts. Bring it with the declarations.

I looked for a dollar cap on the pane. On this edition I did not find one. The fragile-articles limitation, the one that restricts broken glassware, statuary, marble, chinaware, and porcelain to specified causes of loss or building-glass breakage, says the restriction does not apply to glass that is part of the exterior or interior of a building or structure. The deductible still comes off. The form does not pay for loss or damage in one occurrence until the amount exceeds the deductible shown in the declarations, and then it pays the excess up to the limit. A glass job under that deductible is yours. If your declarations show a glass limit, or a glass deductible, that number is the one I will use. I will not invent a glass cap and call it the form.

Boarding the hole is its own additional coverage, and it is smaller than people think. Glass expenses pays for temporary plates or boarding if repair or replacement of damaged glass is delayed, and for the cost to remove or replace obstructions when repairing or replacing glass that is part of a building. It does not include removing or replacing window displays. The holiday scene in the window is not the pane. If you own that display, and it is property used in the business, it is business personal property. The pane and the display are two inventories. Photograph both before anyone sweeps.

The speaker, if the restaurant owns it and uses it in the business, is business personal property located in the building. Theft by a stranger is a covered cause on the edition I opened, subject to the deductible, the limit, and the exclusions that do apply. A phone in a cook’s pocket is a different sentence. The personal-effects extension applies to personal effects of you, your officers, partners, members, managers, and employees, up to $2,500 at each described premises, and it does not apply to loss or damage by theft. It also does not apply to tools or equipment used in the business. A house phone that belongs to the restaurant is a conversation about business personal property. A phone that belongs to the closer is the personal-effects sentence, and theft is outside it. I am not assigning the Arcadia phone. I was not there.

A countertop terminal is business personal property if you own it and use it. An oven or a walk-in that is permanently installed machinery is inside the building definition, if you insure the building. The same equipment, paid for by a tenant who cannot legally remove it, is the improvements sentence. A blender you can pick up is business personal property. I will read the invoice and the lease before I pick the line. The data on the terminal is a further line. Electronic data, other than data that runs the building’s elevator, lighting, heating, ventilation, air conditioning, or security system, is property not covered except the electronic-data additional coverage. On this edition the most that additional coverage pays for all loss in one policy year is $10,000, unless a higher limit is shown in the declarations. The cards in the terminal are not this paragraph. They are the note on the register.

The commercial property twin I opened is CP 00 10 10 12, with CP 10 30 10 12 when special causes of loss are shown. Special means direct physical loss unless excluded or limited. Money is property not covered on that building form, in a list that names currency, money, notes, and securities. There is no businessowners-style sentence that puts exterior building glass into a tenant’s business personal property just because the building limit is blank. Tenant’s improvements are there. The glass the landlord owns is still a lease question. On the special form, building materials and supplies not attached as part of the building are not paid when the cause is theft, with two exceptions: materials you hold for sale, unless they are on a builders-risk form, and business income or extra expense. A stack of replacement doors in the alley, not yet hung, is the shape of that limitation. The door that is already in the frame is not.

The till is the other note

Money, on BP 00 03 07 13, means currency, coins, and bank notes in current use and having a face value, and traveler’s checks, register checks, and money orders held for sale to the public. Securities are a separate definition, and they are not money. Money or securities are property not covered, except as provided in the money-and-securities optional coverage or the employee-dishonesty optional coverage. The building limit is not a drawer limit. The safe ABC7 described is a piece of property. The currency inside it is this paragraph.

Money and securities, if the optional coverage is on the declarations, pays for money and securities used in the business while at a bank or savings institution, in your living quarters or the living quarters of a partner or an employee who has use and custody, at the described premises, or in transit between any of those places, from theft, disappearance, or destruction. Theft here means any act of stealing. One occurrence is limited to the inside-the-premises limit while the money is at the described premises or at a bank, and the outside-the-premises limit while it is anywhere else. You have to keep records so the amount can be verified. A memory of the drawer, on Thursday, is not that record. The form also will not pay, under this optional coverage, for money in a money-operated device unless the amount deposited is recorded by a continuous recording instrument in the device. Write the drop down the same night.

If an optional-coverage deductible is shown, the most the form deducts in one occurrence from money and securities, employee dishonesty, outdoor signs, and forgery or alteration, taken together, is that optional deductible. It does not increase the property deductible. It goes toward satisfying the property deductible. I will read the declarations. I will not assume the two numbers are the same.

If the hand on the cash is on the payroll, stop and read the other note. The dishonesty exclusion on this form takes theft by you, by partners, members, officers, managers, employees including temporary or leased employees, directors, trustees, authorized representatives, and by anyone you entrust the property to. It applies whether or not the act happens during your normal hours. Destruction by employees is not the exclusion. Theft by employees is still not covered, except the employee-dishonesty optional coverage if it is on the declarations. The night drop, the forged check, the counterfeit hundred, and the crime form are the note I wrote on November 16. I am not teaching them again. This note is the stranger.

A stranger and a brick is this note. A closer and a void is the other one.

The sign is a smaller number

On BP 00 03 07 13, the most the form pays for loss of or damage to outdoor signs attached to buildings is $1,000 per sign in any one occurrence. That is a limit, in the limits section. Signs that are not attached to buildings are property not covered, except the outdoor-property extension or the outdoor-signs optional coverage.

The outdoor-property extension applies to outdoor fences, radio and television antennas including satellite dishes, signs other than signs attached to buildings, trees, shrubs, and plants, including debris removal. The causes are fire, lightning, explosion, riot or civil commotion, and aircraft. Vandalism is not on that list. Theft is not on that list. The most the extension pays is $2,500, unless a higher outdoor-property limit is shown in the declarations, and not more than $1,000 for any one tree, shrub, or plant. An A-frame on the sidewalk, tagged or stolen, is this extension only if the cause is one of those five. A holiday chalkboard at the curb is a sign. It is not the building limit.

Outdoor signs, the optional coverage, pays for direct physical loss of or damage to all outdoor signs at the described premises that you own, or that you have in your care, custody, or control. The covered-causes paragraph and the exclusions do not apply to it, except governmental action, nuclear hazard, and war and military action. It still will not pay for wear and tear, hidden or latent defect, rust, corrosion, or mechanical breakdown. The limit is whatever is shown in the declarations for outdoor signs. The optional coverage supersedes all other references to outdoor signs in the policy. If that line is on your declarations, the $1,000 and the five-cause list are not the sign answer. If the line is blank, they are.

On CP 00 10 10 12 the sign number is different, and I do not want a businessowners cap pasted onto a property form. The most that form pays for outdoor signs, whether or not the sign is attached to a building, is $2,500 per sign in any one occurrence. Its outdoor-property extension, which is fences, antennas, trees, shrubs, and plants, is $1,000, and not more than $250 for any one tree, shrub, or plant, and the causes are the same five, if they are covered causes of loss. Signs are not in that extension’s list. They are in the limits sentence. Which form you bought is the declarations. I will not average $1,000 and $2,500 into a sign.

The nights you cannot open

The seats you cannot sell are business income, if that coverage is on the policy. On this businessowners edition it is an additional coverage, and it is not subject to the property limits. The form pays the actual loss of business income you sustain from the necessary suspension of your operations during the period of restoration. The suspension has to be caused by direct physical loss of or damage to property at the described premises, from a covered cause of loss. Suspension means the partial slowdown or complete cessation of your business activities, or that part or all of the described premises is rendered untenantable. Business income means the net income, net profit or loss before income taxes, that would have been earned or incurred if no physical loss or damage had occurred, not including extra income from a boom caused by the loss itself, plus continuing normal operating expenses, including payroll.

The period of restoration begins 72 hours after the time of the direct physical loss for business income, and it begins immediately for extra expense. It ends on the earlier of the date the property should be repaired, rebuilt, or replaced with reasonable speed and similar quality, or the date business is resumed at a new permanent location. The form pays business income you sustain during that period and within 12 consecutive months after the date of the loss. Ordinary payroll, which is everyone except officers, executives, department managers, employees under contract, and anyone the declarations add to that list, is limited to 60 days following the date of the loss, unless the declarations show more days. That payroll still sits inside business income, so the 72-hour wait applies to it. Extended business income, if the suspension produced a payable business-income loss, can run up to 60 more consecutive days after the property is repaired and operations resume, unless the declarations show more, and it does not pay for unfavorable business conditions the loss caused in the area.

No dollar deductible applies to business income or to extra expense on this edition. The 72 hours are the wait. A door that is boarded Saturday and a dining room that opens Sunday can sit inside those 72 hours, and the income line has nothing to pay for the hours that did. Extra expense can still be there, because it begins immediately: the cost you incur, that you would not have incurred, to avoid or minimize the suspension and keep operating, or to minimize the suspension if you cannot. The glass company’s invoice is a property claim, subject to the property deductible. A rush delivery that lets you open, if it is extra expense, is the other line. I want both invoices. I will not call the second one the first.

On a commercial property policy, business income is often its own form. I walked the 72 hours on CP 00 30 10 12 in the note on the hood. A carrier can type a different number of hours. The number on your schedule is the number.

The first 72 hours of lost seats, on this edition, are yours. The board over the door can still be a property claim.

What takes the claim off the form

Vacancy is the long close, and a holiday week is not it. On BP 00 03 07 13, if the building has been vacant for more than 60 consecutive days before the loss, the form will not pay for vandalism, sprinkler leakage unless you protected the system against freezing, building glass breakage, water damage, theft, or attempted theft, even if they are otherwise covered causes. For other covered causes, the amount otherwise payable is reduced by 15 percent. When the policy is issued to a tenant, building means the unit or suite you lease, and it is vacant when it does not contain enough business personal property to conduct customary operations. When it is issued to the owner or general lessee, the building is the entire building, and it is vacant unless at least 31 percent of the square footage is rented and used for customary operations, or used by the owner for customary operations. Buildings under construction or renovation are not considered vacant.

A dark week between Christmas and New Year’s, with the line still in the kitchen, is not the sentence I just read. Sixty consecutive days, and not enough property left to run the restaurant, is. A remodel is the renovation sentence, which the form says is not vacant. I still want to know the dates before anyone tells you a boarded storefront was fine. CP 00 10 10 12 uses the same 60 days, the same 31 percent, the same list of causes the form will not pay, and the same 15 percent. If the property policy is the California FAIR Plan, stop and read the note on the plan. This businessowners form is the wrong page to lay on top of it.

Two limitations look like a theft exclusion and are narrower than the rumor. Property that is missing, where the only evidence is a shortage disclosed on taking inventory, or where there is no physical evidence to show what happened, is not paid. That limitation does not apply to the money-and-securities optional coverage. A broken door is physical evidence. A drawer that is light, and a door that is whole, is the shortage sentence, and it may also be the employee note. For theft, furs are $2,500, jewelry and precious metals including silver are $2,500, with an exception for jewelry and watches worth $100 or less per item, and patterns, dies, molds, and forms are $2,500, unless the declarations show a higher limit. Stainless is not a word I read in that list. Real silver flatware is. I will not call a prep table a precious metal.

I looked for the word unattended. It is not in BP 00 03 07 13, and it is not in CP 00 10 10 12. What I did find is specific. On the property form, the off-premises extension does not apply to property in or on a vehicle, or in the care of your salespersons except at a fair, trade show, or exhibition, and the most it pays is $10,000. On the businessowners form, business personal property includes property in the open or in a vehicle within 100 feet of the building or of the described premises, whichever is greater. The off-premises extension there is covered property, other than money, securities, valuable papers, and accounts receivable, in transit or at a premises you do not own, lease, or operate, and the most it pays is $10,000. I did not read a vehicle exclusion on that businessowners extension. A carrier can add one. If an unattended-vehicle exclusion is on your forms list, it is yours. I will not paste one in from a story I heard at a bar.

False pretense is the person you handed the speaker to. Voluntary parting, induced by a fraudulent scheme, trick, device, or false pretense, is excluded. A brick does not ask. The alarm endorsement is the other way a theft comes off the form, and it is not the fire endorsement.

The protective-safeguards form I can open is CP 04 11 09 17. It is a condition that you maintain the scheduled safeguards you control in complete working order, that you keep any automatic fire alarm or other automatic system listed in the schedule actively engaged and in the on position at all times, and that you notify the insurer if you know of a suspension or impairment. The exclusion it adds is fire: the insurer will not pay for loss or damage caused by or resulting from fire if, prior to the fire, you failed to comply with any of those conditions. The symbols include a security service, P-3, a guard with a recording system or a watch clock making hourly rounds when the premises are not in operation. The exclusion sentence is still fire. I walked the cooking symbol, P-5, in the hood note. I am not turning a burglar-alarm conversation into that fire exclusion.

The theft version, for a businessowners policy, is a different endorsement. FC&S, on December 1, 2025, describes BP 06 49 08 24, Burglary and Robbery Protective Safeguards. I have not opened a specimen of that form. I am using FC&S’s description, and the schedule on your policy is the page that counts. FC&S says the endorsement is used when protective devices or services are a condition of coverage, and that the company has to be notified if the devices are discontinued or out of service. It says paragraph A requires that an automatic burglary alarm, or other automatic system listed in the schedule, be actively engaged and kept in the on position during all non-work hours and whenever the premises are unoccupied. The schedule can require more, including protection during business hours. The notice exception FC&S describes is narrow: notification is not necessary for a suspension or impairment of that alarm if protection can be restored within 48 hours and you provide at least one watchperson or other means of surveillance during non-work hours and whenever the premises are otherwise unoccupied. An alarm that was never turned on at close is the sentence FC&S says the form was written to reach. Read the exclusion on your copy before anyone tells you the fire form and the theft form are the same page.

Nine hundred fifty dollars is a statute

People say the law changed, and then they ask whether the policy now pays because a theft can be charged differently. It does not. I am not your prosecutor. The declarations do not move when the Penal Code does.

Penal Code section 490.2 says obtaining property by theft where the value does not exceed $950 is petty theft and is punished as a misdemeanor, with the exceptions in that section. Section 459.5 defines shoplifting as entering a commercial establishment with intent to commit larceny while it is open during regular business hours, where the value taken or intended does not exceed $950. The next sentence is the one I want on a 4:45 a.m. door: any other entry into a commercial establishment with intent to commit larceny is burglary. Section 459 is the burglary statute itself. Entering a shop or a store, among other places, with intent to commit grand or petit larceny or any felony is burglary. A closed restaurant and a brick is that file. It is not the open-hours shoplifting definition.

Proposition 36 added section 666.1, effective December 18, 2024. A person with two or more prior convictions for the offenses that section lists, and the list includes petty theft, burglary, robbery, and shoplifting, who is convicted of petty theft or shoplifting, is punishable in the county jail for up to one year or under section 1170(h). A second or subsequent conviction of that section can be county jail or state prison. The same initiative added section 12022.65, also effective December 18, 2024. A person who acts in concert with two or more other persons to take, attempt to take, damage, or destroy property, in the commission or attempted commission of a felony, faces an additional and consecutive term of one, two, or three years. It is not imposed unless the facts are charged and admitted or found true. Those are charging statutes. They do not type a money limit, and they do not shorten the 72 hours.

The close, and the morning after

This is the order I want, while the door is still whole, and again if you are standing in the glass.

  1. Empty the drawer before you arm the alarm. Currency is not covered property on the edition I opened unless the money-and-securities line, or employee dishonesty, is on the declarations. The inside limit and the outside limit are two numbers. The walk to the bank, and the employee, are the other note. A drop does not create a limit. It shortens the time the cash sits where a brick can reach it. Write the amount down. The optional coverage wants records that can verify the loss.
  2. Arm what the schedule names. If BP 06 49, or a carrier’s burglary form, is on the policy, FC&S’s description wants the alarm on during non-work hours and whenever the place is unoccupied. Turn it on at close. If you know it is down, the notice condition is the one on your form. The 48-hour pass FC&S describes still wants a person watching, or some other surveillance, while it is down. A camera aimed at the door is how the Arcadia, Covina, and Pasadena stories exist at all. I want the light on the glass so the picture is a picture. I am not quoting a study. I am telling you what I want at a restaurant that closes after dark in December.
  3. If the glass is already down, call the police. On both forms I opened, the duties say you must see that the police are notified if a law may have been broken. Do it the same morning. I am not putting a department’s number on this page. Use the number your city already gave you. If someone is still inside, that is an emergency, and you already know how to reach one. Then call me.
  4. Photograph before the board goes up. The door, the glass, the safe, the drawer, the sign, the display. The duties want prompt notice, with a description of the property, and as soon as possible how, when, and where. If it is feasible, set the damaged property aside so it can be examined. Keep a record of what you spend to protect it. At their request, inventory the damaged and undamaged property, with quantities, costs, values, and the amount claimed. A signed, sworn proof of loss, with the information they ask for, is due within 60 days after they request it. They supply the forms. Cooperate. Resume operations as quickly as possible. Your memory on Thursday is not the inventory.
  5. Then the declarations, in this order. Is the form a businessowners policy or a commercial property policy, and is the property piece the FAIR Plan. Is theft still a covered cause when you reach the end of the exclusions. Who insures the glass and the door, you or the landlord. Is there a glass limit or a glass deductible I should be using instead of the building limit. What is the property deductible. For signs, is outdoor signs optional coverage on the declarations, or are we on the $1,000 attached-sign cap, or on the property form’s $2,500. Is money and securities shown, and what are the inside and outside limits. Is business income on the policy, and is the waiting period 72 hours or a number someone typed. Is a burglary protective-safeguards endorsement attached, and was the alarm on. Is the building inside the vacancy sentence. A photo of the declarations is enough to start.
