The new year is not a new license.

Six California changes I could verify before a 2027 renewal. A Type 48 keeps the drink lids. A new server’s class adds drink spiking. A service window has a size rule. Leave, a quit fee, and a July scheduling rule are the employment side. The wage and the to-go cocktail are already written.

By Sam Johnson, account executive at Shaw, Moses, Mendenhall & Associates. I am not your licensing lawyer. I place the policy that has to live with the license.

The text this week was the pile. An owner in Arcadia had heard that “a bunch of laws change on January 1” and wanted to know which of them belong on the renewal, and which of them are somebody else’s headline. I went to the chaptered bills. I am keeping the ones I could read on the legislature’s site, or on the department that enforces them.

Three of them already have their own notes, and I am not reprinting them. The January wage, and what it does to a workers’ comp estimate, is the payroll note. Cocktails to-go through December 31, 2029 are the November 6 note. A comment about someone’s papers, the civil penalty in AB 2495, and the employment-practices form, are the October note. The 120-day pop-up storefront is the storefront note. The workers’ comp benchmark, and the fact that I did not find a January 1, 2027 pure premium rate filing, are the burn note.

What is left is the list below. A statute enacted at a regular session, with no urgency clause, goes into effect on January 1 next following a 90-day period from the date of enactment. That sentence is Article IV, section 8 of the California Constitution. Where a bill names a different date, I use the bill.

The statute is not the form. Read both, and do not let one stand in for the other.

The Type 48 lid does not come off in January

This one is a bar license, not a dining-room license. Assembly Bill 1982 (Lowenthal, Chapter 377, Statutes of 2026) was approved by the Governor on September 20, 2026. It amends Business and Professions Code sections 25624 and 25625. The old text repealed those drink-spiking rules on January 1, 2027. The chaptered bill takes the sunset out. The legislative counsel’s digest says that repeal of the sunset indefinitely extends the requirements. The bill has no urgency clause, so the constitution’s January 1 date is the date it takes effect. That is the same morning the old sunset would have arrived.

The Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control’s October 9, 2026 advisory says the sunset has been repealed and the protocols will not self-repeal. Type 48 licensees, including a premises on an interim operating permit, keep operating under those two sections. If your license is a Type 41 or a Type 47, this section is not your section. The eating-place license, and the liquor form that has to live with it, are the Type 41 and Type 47 note.

What the Type 48 still has to do is already the daily rule. I am writing it here so nobody treats New Year’s Eve as the day the kits can come off the shelf.

  • Offer drug testing devices to customers. The statute says test strips, stickers, straws, and other devices designed to detect a controlled substance in a drink, and it names flunitrazepam, ketamine, and GHB as examples, not as a closed list. Sell them at no more than a reasonable amount based on the wholesale cost, or give them away. ABC’s questions on that advisory say one device does not have to detect all three substances. A device that detects at least one controlled substance used to drug a person through a drink, such as GHB, ketamine, or flunitrazepam, is what the department calls compliant. The devices you offer have to be inside the expiration date or the recommended period of use on the label, the package, or the manufacturer’s instructions.
  • On request, provide a lid with the drink. The statute defines a lid as a removable cover, of any size, that attaches to the rim. It has to fit at least one container you serve in. It does not have to fit every glass. ABC says a napkin is not a lid. You may charge a fee that does not exceed a reasonable amount based on the wholesale cost, or you may give the lid away. If the guest asks and the lid fits a different cup than the one you poured, serve that drink in the cup the lid fits. That last instruction is the department’s, on the same advisory.
  • Post one of the two notices the statute prints, in a prominent and conspicuous location. ABC says the words have to be verbatim, and the sign has to be clearly visible and readable to patrons who are there. The department posts printable versions. Use those, or the sentences in the bill. Do not tape up a paraphrase, including mine.

A few limits, because they get told backwards. Section 25624 says a Type 48 licensee shall not be held liable for a defective test or an inaccurate result, including a false positive or a false negative. That sentence is about the test. It is not a sentence about the drink. Section 25624 and section 25625 both say a violation is not a crime. Section 25625 adds that a sole violation of the lid section, including the notice, results only in a warning from the department for the first offense. I am not stretching that warning over the test-kit section. ABC says a violation of these laws is not a criminal offense and may still result in administrative action against the license.

On the policy, those are two files. The sign and the kit are the department. A guest who says the drink was tampered with is a person. General liability sends the pour itself off to the liquor form, if you bought one. I walked that split, for a fight, in the New Year’s Eve note. A spiked drink is not that fight, and I am not going to tell you the liquor form answers it. Read the liquor form for that fact. An ABC warning, or an accusation against the license, is not a claim check. Posting the sign does not add a limit to the declarations page.

The class changes. The card already in a pocket stays.

The course content is older than this fall’s bills, and the date inside it is still January 1. Business and Professions Code section 25681 was amended by Assembly Bill 2402, Chapter 829 of the Statutes of 2024. The code page says that amendment was effective January 1, 2025. Subdivision (f) says that commencing on or before January 1, 2027, responsible beverage service courses shall include best practices on how to prevent or protect a person from drink spiking, and best practices if the person believes they, or someone they know, have been drugged that way. The statute defines drink spiking as putting alcohol or drugs into another person’s drink without their knowing and express consent.

The department’s RBS questions put the work on the training companies. Curriculum updates have to be submitted and approved no later than December 31, 2026. A provider that does not have that approval will not be permitted to conduct training after January 1, 2027. The same page says the updated curriculum includes drink-spiking prevention and the appropriate actions when a patron may be a victim.

A card that is already valid does not die on New Year’s morning because the outline changed. Section 25681 says an alcohol server certification is valid for three years and is valid for any person employed by a licensee subject to the article. ABC says the certification follows the person, not one employer, and that a server who already holds a valid certification does not certify again just to work for you. A renewal is a different errand. ABC says you can renew within 90 days of the expiration date, and the renewal is training from an approved provider plus the department’s exam. Training taken after January 1 has to be from a provider still allowed to teach, which is a provider whose updated curriculum was approved.

A new hire is on the clock the department already uses. ABC says alcohol servers and their managers have 60 days from the first day of employment to register, take the training, and pass the exam, and the exam itself is within 30 days of the confirmed training. Someone you put on the floor in January takes the course that includes the new material. The department’s list of license types subject to RBS includes a Type 41, a Type 47, and a Type 48. A café that does not pour is not in that sentence.

The card is a license condition. ABC says a server’s failure to be certified is not a crime, and that the licensee may still face administrative action if the servers are out of compliance. The card is not the assault wording. If two guests fight, or someone on the clock steps in, that is still the form in the New Year’s Eve note. A current certification does not fill in a sublimit the declarations left blank.

A service window is a health-code sentence

Senate Bill 918 (Seyarto, Chapter 988, Statutes of 2026) was approved on September 30, 2026. It amends Health and Safety Code section 114259.2. No urgency clause. January 1, 2027.

This is a pass-through or another service opening. A dining room in Sierra Madre with a front door and no window onto a lane can skip the measurements. If you hand food through an opening, the section is the one the inspector is already using, with the sizes rewritten.

  • An opening of no more than 216 square inches still has to be at least 18 inches from the next one, with a solid or screened window that is closed when it is not in use. Screening is at least 16 mesh per square inch. The counter surface is smooth and easily cleanable. The digest says the bill removes the old requirement that this smaller window be equipped with a self-closing device.
  • An opening between 216 and 432 square inches has to have an air curtain or a self-closing device. The counter is still smooth and easily cleanable.
  • An opening larger than 432 square inches has to have both a self-closing device and an air curtain. If there is a counter, it is smooth and easily cleanable.
  • A pass-through window or other service opening is for food delivery. It is not a general entrance or exit for employees or customers.

The digest says the Retail Food Code is enforced primarily by local public health agencies, and that a violation of the code is a misdemeanor unless another provision says otherwise. It also says this bill expands the scope of that existing crime. I am not predicting a charge. I am telling you the opening is something an inspector can write up.

The air curtain is a piece of equipment. It is not a limit on the declarations page. A citation is not a claim. If the department tapes the door, or shuts the window and the night goes with it, that closure is the question I already walked in the food-contamination note. A guest who says the meal made them sick is general liability. A food-contamination endorsement, if you bought one, has its own trigger. A failed inspection is often neither.

Five days, for a person the employee names

Senate Bill 1149 (Durazo, Chapter 679, Statutes of 2026) was approved on September 27, 2026. It amends Government Code section 12945.7. No urgency clause. January 1, 2027.

The leave itself is not new. An employer of five or more people already had to allow up to five days when a family member died. Beginning January 1, family member also includes a designated person: anyone related to the employee by blood, or whose association with the employee is the equivalent of a family relationship. The employee may name that person at the time they ask for the leave. You may limit each employee to one designated person in a 12-month period.

The person has to have worked for you at least 30 days before the leave starts. The five days do not have to be consecutive. They have to be finished within three months of the death. If you ask, the employee provides documentation within 30 days of the first day of the leave. The statute’s examples are a death certificate, a published obituary, or written verification of the death, burial, or memorial from a mortuary, funeral home, burial society, crematorium, religious institution, or governmental agency. You keep that paper confidential. It goes to internal personnel or counsel, as necessary, or where the law requires it. It does not go on the line.

Pay follows the bereavement policy you already have. If you have no policy, the days may be unpaid, and the employee may use vacation, personal leave, accrued and available sick leave, or compensatory time. If your policy pays fewer than five days, the employee still gets five days total. The days the policy pays are paid. The rest may be unpaid, and the same accrued time can be used. Refusing the leave, or firing, demoting, fining, suspending, or otherwise discriminating because the person took it or testified about it, is an unlawful employment practice under this section. So is interfering with the right.

A café with fewer than five people paid a wage or salary is outside this section’s definition of employer. A house with a collective bargaining agreement should read subdivision (k) before assuming the statute sits on top of the contract. The exception applies when the agreement expressly provides bereavement leave equivalent to this section, covers wages, hours, and working conditions, pays premium overtime where that applies, and pays a regular hourly rate at least 30 percent above the state minimum wage.

The five days are a schedule. They are not a workers’ comp claim, and they are not the payroll estimate in the wage note. Refusing them is an employment claim. That form, and the wage claims it usually does not indemnify, are the October note. I am not going to tell you from Huntington Drive that your employment-practices page picks up this leave. If the page is missing, the lawsuit does not grow a policy.

A training bill on a contract dated January 1

Assembly Bill 1697 (Kalra, Chapter 889, Statutes of 2026) was approved on September 30, 2026. It is an urgency statute, so it took effect that day. What it moved is the date of the contract. Business and Professions Code section 16608 and Labor Code section 926, as amended, apply the prohibition to contracts entered into on or after January 1, 2027. The same sections say the version that existed on January 1, 2026 is inoperative from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026. The bill’s findings say the amendments prevent liability from accruing for acts between January 1, 2026 and the effective date of the act, and that a pending claim alleged to have occurred in that stretch is moot.

For a contract signed on or after January 1, it is unlawful to include a term, or to require a worker to sign as a condition of the job a term, that does any of three things if the job ends: makes the worker pay the employer, a training provider, or a debt collector for a debt; lets that person start collecting, or end a forbearance, because the job ended; or imposes a penalty, fee, or cost. The statute’s examples of that last bucket include a retraining fee, a replacement fee, and a quit fee. A contract that violates the section is void under Business and Professions Code section 16600 only if it was entered into on or after January 1, 2027. A worker, or a worker’s representative, may sue. A person found liable owes the actual damages, or $5,000 per worker, whichever is greater, plus injunctive relief and reasonable attorney’s fees and costs.

The line I care about in a kitchen is the one that says a cook owes you the “training” if they leave in six months. That is the term to take off a contract dated January 1 or later. The statute has exceptions, and I am not going to flatten them into “no bonuses.” Subdivision (b)(2) is the list. It includes certain government loan and grant programs, a separate contract for tuition toward a transferable credential, an approved apprenticeship, a discretionary bonus that is not tied to specific job performance, and a repayment of advanced paid time off, up to 40 hours, when the worker leaves voluntarily. The bonus path has its own conditions: a separate agreement, at least five business days to consult a lawyer, no interest, proration, a retention period that does not exceed two years, an option to wait until the end of that period and owe nothing, and a separation that was the employee’s choice or a termination for misconduct. Read the subdivision before you keep a repayment paragraph. A slogan from me is not the exception.

A lawsuit over that contract is an employment lawsuit. It lands on the employment-practices page if you have one. The $5,000 figure is a statute. It is not a limit I can point to on a declarations page, and I am not going to promise the form pays it. The October note is where I describe the wage gap on that form. Bring the agreement and the employment-practices page. I will read both.

July 1 is the scheduling score

This one does not arrive on New Year’s Day. Senate Bill 947 (McNerney, Chapter 859, Statutes of 2026), the bill that adds Labor Code sections 1520 through 1526.7, was approved on September 30, 2026. Section 1526.7 says the part becomes operative on July 1, 2027. I am putting it in a January note because a renewal you bind this winter will still be in force on that morning.

An automated decision system, in section 1520, is a computational process from machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or artificial intelligence that issues a simplified output, including a score, a classification, or a recommendation, used to assist or replace a human’s discretionary decision, and that materially affects a natural person. The same definition says it does not include a spam filter, a firewall, antivirus software, an identity and access management tool, a calculator, a database, a dataset, or another compilation of data. I will not stand on a sidewalk in Glendora and call your time clock illegal. A grid of shifts can be a dataset. A score that you use to write someone up or let them go is the sentence to read.

Starting July 1, an employer shall not use that kind of system to violate a labor, safety, employment, or civil-rights law, to infer a protected status under the Fair Employment and Housing Act, or to predict that a worker will exercise a legal right and then take adverse action for it. You shall not rely solely on the system for a disciplinary decision or a termination. If you primarily rely on its output for one of those decisions, a human has to corroborate it, using the data that produced the output or other supporting information. The statute’s examples include a manager’s evaluation, the personnel file, the person’s work, a peer review, and witness interviews, which may include relevant online customer reviews. If you cannot corroborate the output, or the reviewer decides it is inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading, you do not use it for that decision.

When you did primarily rely on it, you give the person a written notice at the time you tell them the decision. Plain language. Its own page, not a sentence buried in the write-up. In the language you ordinarily use with the crew. Email, a link, or another written form. The notice says you primarily relied on the system, that a human reviewed it and corroborated it, how to reach a human, and that retaliation for using these rights is prohibited. The employee may ask for a meaningful, objective description of their own data that the system used. Other people’s information in that description has to be anonymized.

There is no five-employee floor in the definition of employer. It reaches a person who employs someone or controls wages, hours, or working conditions, and it includes a labor contractor. The Labor Commissioner may enforce it. A public prosecutor may too. The civil penalty in the bill is $500 per violation. Retaliation for using the right, or for complaining, is its own prohibition. A collective bargaining agreement can set the part aside only if the waiver is clear, the agreement covers the work, and it provides protection from algorithmic management.

A firing is the employment-practices conversation, which is the October note. The $500 is a civil penalty in the Labor Code. I am not going to tell you a policy pays penalties because the word “employment” is on the dec page. Read the form. If the tool you use in June is a score, decide before July who the human is, and write the notice before you need it.

What I would read before you renew

None of this replaces the license, the health permit, or the handbook. It is the stack I would put on the table with the declarations page.

  1. Read the license type before you read the lid rule. A Type 48 keeps the kits, the lids, and one of the two notices. A Type 41 or a Type 47 does not pick up that statute by being open late. The liquor form is a separate page. The fight, if the night becomes one, is the New Year’s Eve note.
  2. Look up the RBS cards that expire in the next 90 days. A valid card stays valid. A renewal trained after January 1, and a hire you make in January, sit in the course that includes drink spiking. The card is ABC’s. It is not a coverage grant.
  3. If food goes out a window, measure the opening. 216 square inches, between 216 and 432, or larger than 432, and then the hardware the section names. The window is for handing food out. It is not the door the crew uses. A failed inspection is often neither general liability nor a food-contamination endorsement.
  4. Write the designated person into the leave you already give. Five days, one designated person per 12 months if you use that limit, documentation if you ask for it, and confidentiality. Fewer than five employees is outside the section. Refusing the leave is an employment claim.
  5. Take a training repayment off any agreement you will have someone sign on or after January 1. A bonus, tuition, or advanced paid time off can remain when the contract meets section 16608. The $5,000 figure is the statute’s floor for damages in that lawsuit, or the actual damages if those are higher. It is not a price, and it is not a limit on your policy.
  6. Put July 1 on the same calendar as the renewal. If a tool scores a person and you would discipline or fire from that score, a human has to be able to corroborate it, and the notice has to be ready. A time clock that only records the punch is not the system I am describing.
  7. Then the two notes I am not repeating. Put the January wage into the workers’ comp payroll estimate. That arithmetic is the payroll note. If to-go cocktails are on the menu, the meal, the seal, and the pickup are the November 6 note, and the question for me is whether the liquor form was written for a drink that leaves.

If you would rather do this over a plate of food than over six bills, pick the restaurant. Bring the license, the handbook page on leave, any new-hire agreement you plan to use in January, and the declarations page. I’ll buy.
