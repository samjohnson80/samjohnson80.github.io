The California restaurant insurance market, as I see it this fall

A national survey says property got cheaper. A letter from your carrier may say something else. Both can be true. Here is how I sort them.

By Sam Johnson, account executive at Shaw, Moses, Mendenhall & Associates. Written for owners, not for insurance people.

The question usually shows up before the food does. “Is it just me, or did this get ugly?” Sometimes the owner is holding a non-renewal. Sometimes the premium moved and the letter explaining it was two paragraphs. Sometimes it is both.

I would rather walk the actual documents than hand you a mood. This is the fall 2026 version, for a restaurant, bar, or café in the San Gabriel Valley. Monrovia, Pasadena, Arcadia, Duarte, Azusa, Glendora, Sierra Madre, San Gabriel. The foothill addresses and the Huntington Drive addresses are not the same risk, and I will not pretend they are.

The national average is a real number. It is not your building.

If you only catch a headline, you will think commercial property got cheaper this year. The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers surveyed brokers on policies from April through June 2026. Across all account sizes, premiums were down 2.0% on average. That followed a decrease in the first quarter, the first time the all-sizes number had fallen since 2017. Commercial property fell the most of any line, 6.3%, and it was the second quarter in a row that property was the biggest drop. Workers’ compensation in that same survey was down 3.2%, the eighteenth quarter in a row of decreases for that line. Umbrella went the other way, up 5.3%, the thirty-fifth quarter in a row and the biggest increase of the quarter. Commercial auto was next, up 4.5%. Seventy-five percent of the brokers who answered said property capacity had increased. Leader’s Edge wrote up the same survey.

Read the next sentence slowly. That survey is national. It covers every kind of business the brokers happen to place. It is not a survey of dining rooms in Pasadena. The Council’s write-up says carriers eased property pricing partly to offset the lines that would not ease, umbrella and auto among them, and that a given account still turns on location, construction, occupancy, and loss history. A café on a flat block in Arcadia and a place sitting against the brush above Sierra Madre can both sell coffee and live in different markets.

I will believe a market is back when I can place the building, not when a press release says someone intends to try.

California property, and the companies that stepped back

The California Department of Insurance has described this in its own words. When Commissioner Lara approved higher commercial limits on the FAIR Plan in March 2025, the department said traditional insurers had retreated from high-risk areas, and that the higher limits were for people, business owners included, “who are being priced out or left without coverage altogether.” The FAIR Plan is the insurer of last resort. It grew because the regular market stepped back. That is the department talking, not a sales line.

What he approved: Division I commercial property limits of $20 million per building, and $100 million per location. The plan had to make those limits available within 120 days of the March 28, 2025 approval. A 2026 stipulation between the department and the FAIR Plan says the high-value commercial policies began on July 26, 2025. The 2024 order that set this up says the higher limits sunset three years after the plan starts offering them. So this is a bridge. It is not a new permanent home for the building.

If you land there, read what you actually bought. The plan publishes a sample commercial policy. The coverage grant is direct physical loss to covered property from the causes of loss the form names. That is a property policy. It is not general liability, it is not liquor liability, and it is not workers’ comp. On its difference-in-conditions page, the plan says it does not sell the policies people buy to fill gaps such as water damage, theft, and liability. That page is written around a homeowners policy, so do not stretch it into a promise about your restaurant. The practical point is narrower and enough: if the only property quote left is the FAIR Plan, we still build the rest of the program beside it. Insuring the stove does not insure the guest who falls in front of it.

One credit on that policy is easy to miss. The same Department of Insurance release says residential and commercial FAIR Plan policyholders can get a discount of up to 20 percent on the wildfire portion of the premium for hardening the property. “Up to” is doing work in that sentence. If you are already on the plan, ask about it before you decide the number cannot move.

The department is also trying to pull business back into the regular market. Under the Sustainable Insurance Strategy, insurers that use wildfire catastrophe models the department has reviewed are supposed to write and keep more homeowners and commercial policies in wildfire-prone areas. Mercury, Allstate, and CSAA were the first to say they would file those plans. A filing is not a quote for your address.

If the non-renewal says “wildfire”

Starting January 1, 2026, Senate Bill 547 added Insurance Code section 675.55. For one year after a declared emergency, an insurer cannot cancel or refuse to renew a commercial property policy, when the building sits in a ZIP code inside or next to the fire perimeter, based solely on the fact that a wildfire happened there. The policy had to be in force when the emergency was declared. The commissioner then publishes the ZIP codes. Bulletin 2026-6 is one of those lists. Yours may be a different bulletin. Read the one that matches your fire, not the one you found first.

The protection has edges, and a restaurant can fall off them.

  • It does not apply if the named insured’s willful or grossly negligent acts materially increased the risk.
  • It does not apply if losses unrelated to the fire would make the risk ineligible for renewal.
  • It does not apply if physical changes, beyond the catastrophe damage, have made the property uninsurable.
  • It does not cover a commercial property policy with an annual premium of $25,000 or more when the insured employed at least 25 people on average over the prior 12 months. Habitational policies, apartments and the like, are pulled back into the rule. A restaurant is not an apartment building.

The word that matters is “solely.” A non-renewal for claims, an old roof, or a carrier leaving the class entirely is a different letter. A small place can be inside this protection. A bigger one can be outside it. I would rather read your notice with you than have you guess which one you are holding.

Workers’ comp is on a different chart

Here the California number and the national number disagree, and both can be true, because they are not measuring the same thing. The broker survey above showed workers’ compensation premiums down. The California benchmark went up.

On July 10, 2026, the WCIRB reported that the insurance commissioner had approved advisory pure premium rates for policies incepting on or after September 1, 2026. The approved average is $1.65 per $100 of payroll, a 6.6 percent increase from the approved September 1, 2025 rates. The bureau had proposed an average increase of 10.4 percent. The commissioner approved 6.6. The bureau says the gap is different assumptions about loss development and trend.

An advisory pure premium rate is a benchmark for losses and the cost of handling claims. It is not your premium. The WCIRB’s filing says an insurer is not required to use the pure premium rate when it sets what it charges. Payroll, your experience modification (that is your claims history, turned into a factor), and the carrier’s own filed rate all sit on top. I am not going to tell you your restaurant’s comp bill moved 6.6 percent. I am going to tell you the statewide benchmark did.

For the classes I actually see on restaurant and bar policies, the bureau’s classification pages currently show $2.784 per $100 of payroll as the latest pure premium rate, effective September 1, 2026. A year earlier those pages showed $2.530. The year before that, $2.410.

Kitchens cut, burn, and slip, so these classes sit above the statewide average of $1.65. That is the benchmark saying the claims cost more. It is still not an invoice.

The class is a description of the work, not a vibe. If you brew on site and half or more of that beer is sold for off-site consumption, the bureau says the brewing is a separate classification. If you run a sit-down room and a counter concept in the same operation, you can end up with more than one code, and the payroll has to be split to match. If someone coded a full-service dining room as a counter, or a taproom as a restaurant, the payroll is in the wrong bucket. That is worth ten minutes at renewal.

Liquor liability, and the fight outside

General liability and liquor liability are not the same policy. A fight is often a third conversation, once the endorsements show up. I wrote the license side separately, for owners getting a Type 41 or a Type 47.

The market piece is this. ISO filed assault-or-battery exclusions with a January 1, 2026 edition date. FC&S, in PropertyCasualty360 on October 6, 2025, walked through two of them. CG 40 51 01 26 attaches to the commercial general liability coverage part. CG 35 09 01 26 attaches to the liquor liability coverage part. Where they are attached, the insurance does not apply to injury arising out of an actual, alleged, or threatened assault or battery committed by anyone. The exclusion also reaches an act or omission in preventing or suppressing the fight, and negligence in hiring, training, supervising, or keeping the person involved. FC&S’s point, which matches the form language they quote, is that putting the endorsement on a policy that did not have it makes the coverage smaller.

Not every carrier uses the ISO form. Some policies already excluded this. Some still cover a fight, sometimes only up to a lower limit than the rest of the policy. I am not going to invent a “typical” sublimit. Before you compare two quotes that both say a million dollars, ask whether assault and battery is covered, excluded, or capped, and ask them to show you the page. The certificate will not show it.

What I would actually do in the next month

If the renewal is inside 90 days, this is the order.

  1. Read the notice for the reason and the date coverage ends. “Wildfire” and “we are leaving this class” are different problems, and they have different fixes.
  2. If the reason is solely the wildfire location, check whether your ZIP is in a bulletin under Insurance Code 675.55, and whether the size exception applies to you.
  3. If the property quote in front of you is the FAIR Plan, price the companion policies in the same sitting. Ask about the hardening discount on the wildfire portion if the building can take the work.
  4. On comp, ask which class code is on the policy and what your experience mod is. Bring payroll by job: kitchen, service, bar.
  5. On liquor and general liability, get the assault-and-battery answer in writing.
  6. Bring loss runs, the hood-suppression inspection, and the insurance section of the lease. Underwriters price what they can see.

If you are opening rather than renewing, the list of what to buy first is the other note. And if you would rather do this over a plate of food than over a PDF, that is my preference too.
